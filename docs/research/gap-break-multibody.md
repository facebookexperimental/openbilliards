# gap: 15-ball multibody collision at the break: quantitative comparison of the two approaches' spread-pattern fidelity and the cost of a hybrid architecture (close reading of the Gismeros Moreno paper + pooltool sensitivity experiment)

## KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Gismeros Moreno et al. (Nonlinear Dyn 107:1859–1886, 2022, DOI 10.1007/s11071-021-07117-4) use Lankarani–Nikravesh (ball-ball c_r=0.93) + Hertz (ball-cloth) + LN (ball-cushion c_r=0.98, μ=0.14), n=1.5, but the break simulates only cue+3 balls, 3–4 s of physical time takes 23,613–104,252 s in Matlab, and there is no experimental validation of the 15-ball spread pattern.
- The smooth contact method itself also depends heavily on the initial gap: the paper finds the valid α is only 10⁻⁹–10⁻⁷ m, a too-small gap produces a z-direction energy-gain artifact, and at 2.13×10⁻⁴ m the exit direction is falsified by the non-smooth impulse model—compliant is not a fidelity silver bullet.
- Local pooltool measurement of 40 15-ball breaks: the differences in pocketing rate, mean spread distance and total path across the three orders of magnitude of spacing_factor 1e-4→1e-2 are smaller than the seed variance; the spread distribution is not tuned in by the spacing value, and there is no need to fine-tune the value with footage.
- A single-break result is pure chaos: moving the apex ball by 1 µm shifts final positions by up to 7.4 cm and changes the number of balls pocketed, and at 100 µm the maximum shift is 1.5 m—any scheme can only match distributions, not reproduce a specific shot.
- The corner balls' exit angles are locked at 60°/120° by the rack lattice geometry (sd only 0.02–0.2° at sf≤1e-2), so the two approaches will not diverge much in direction; the divergence is in the energy distribution.
- pooltool's 10% momentum-theft hack (ball_ball/core.py:133, theft_fraction=0.10 @ :188) fires ~290 times/break in 25% of tight-rack (sf≤1e-3) breaks, so the docstring's 'practically a no-op' claim does not hold—this is the real fidelity risk point of the sequential impulse scheme.
- The hybrid architecture is fully feasible computationally (contact duration ~100–300 µs, dt~1–2 µs integrating a 2–5 ms window, compiled implementation <10 ms wall) and the determinism cost under fixed-step is low, but it introduces a calibration burden for K/c_r/contact-phase friction.
- Final recommendation: baseline with sequential impulses + seeded random gaps (nominal gap 0.05–0.5 mm, avoiding the ≤1e-4·R ill-conditioned zone); replace the 10% hack with an iterative frozen-cluster solve; local compliant integration as an optional phase-2 enhancement (K≈3.5×10⁸ N/m^1.5, c_r=0.93), with the event-driven version as the regression oracle.
- One pooltool event-driven 15-ball break takes 252±226 ms wall time (physical duration ~9.3 s, ~870 events), and re-runs with the same (sf, seed) are bitwise identical.

## REPORT
# Comparison of break implementation schemes: sequential pairwise impulses vs nonlinear smooth contact integration

## 1. Close reading of the primary paper: Gismeros Moreno et al., *Nonlinear Dynamics* 107:1859–1886 (2022)

Paper: Raúl Gismeros Moreno, Eduardo Corral Abad, Jesús Meneses Alonso, María Jesús Gómez García, Cristina Castejón Sisamón, "Modelling multiple-simultaneous impact problems with a nonlinear smooth approach: pool/billiard application", published online 2021-12-14, open access, DOI 10.1007/s11071-021-07117-4 (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11071-021-07117-4). All numbers below are taken directly from the full-text PDF (fetched and read closely locally).

### 1.1 Contact force and friction models
- **Ball-ball**: Lankarani–Nikravesh (LN) model, F_N = K·δ^1.5 + χ·δ^1.5·δ̇, χ = 3(1−c_r²)/(4·δ̇⁻)·K; c_r = 0.93 (Table 2). Two friction options: Threlfall constant μ = 0.06, or Alciatore velocity-dependent μ(v) = 9.951×10⁻³ + 0.108·e^(−1.088v).
- **Ball-cloth**: pure Hertz (F_N = K·δ^1.5, on the grounds that the contact is near-permanent and elastic-stretch dissipation is negligible), friction uses the Ambrósio model, μ = 0.2.
- **Ball-cushion**: LN, c_r = 0.85 (frictionless version) or the Mathavan et al. calibrated values c_r = 0.98, μ = 0.14 + Ambrósio friction.
- Exponent n = 1.5; material parameters (Table 1): ball phenol-formaldehyde resin, E = 3800 MPa, ν = 0.375; cushion rubber E = 7 MPa, ν = 0.50; cloth wool E = 3100 MPa, ν = 0.38. The paper gives no K value; from its Eq. 3.4–3.5 with the Table 1 values one can derive ball-ball K ≈ 3.5×10⁸ N/m^1.5 (our derivation, not in the paper). Ball: diameter 57.15 mm, mass 170 g, J = 5.5×10⁻⁵ kg·m².

### 1.2 Scale, time step and computational cost (most critical for us)
- **The break simulates only the cue + the first 3 rack balls, not 15 balls**. Shot speed 10.729 m/s (24 mph).
- Matlab ODE integrator: at the default step of 0.01 s the results are not self-consistent (a symmetric problem shows y-direction drift), and converge only after changing to 1×10⁻⁵ s; the integrator automatically shrinks the step at contact onset.
- Computational cost (Fig. 16, 3–4 s physical duration, only 4 balls): Standard Lagrange 23,613 s; Baumgarte 24,876 s; Augmented Lagrangian @1e-5 s step 80,413 s; Direct correction @1e-5 s 104,252 s. Even the "tuned version" with gravity/cloth normal force removed takes 440–1,730 s. That is, a single 4-ball break runs for hours to a day (Matlab implementation, order-of-magnitude reference).

### 1.3 Initial gap α study (the smooth method also needs "gap tuning")
Center distance d = 2R + α, tested α ∈ {10⁻⁹, 10⁻⁸, 10⁻⁷, 10⁻⁶, 2.1325735×10⁻⁴} m (Table 3):
- α too small: the cue and yellow balls show a non-physical z-direction climb while rolling (energy-gain artifact);
- α = 10⁻⁶: the yellow ball additionally hits the red/blue balls at t≈0.7 s, and the x-direction trajectory changes entirely;
- α = 2.13×10⁻⁴ (random): after the first hit the yellow ball moves along −x, **"This was later checked in a non-smooth model and proved to be wrong"**—i.e. at a large gap the smooth method gives a qualitatively wrong exit direction, and is instead falsified by the impulse method;
- Conclusion: valid range α ∈ 10⁻⁹–10⁻⁷ m. **The smooth contact method is equally highly sensitive to the initial gap; it does not eliminate the "gap tuning" problem, it only pushes the feasible range to a smaller order of magnitude.**

### 1.4 How quantitative the experimental validation is
- Ball-cushion: 2000 frames/s high-speed video (from a third-party videographer), incoming speed 2.724 m/s, the conclusion is only "results almost identical to the real shot" (Fig. 20)—**a visual match, no numerical error metric**; against the Mathavan et al. experimental data, the match is good at low speed, with "slight deviation" at ≥2.5 m/s.
- Ball-ball: against Alciatore's "30° rule" deflection-angle data, "quite similar" at 3 mph, with the maximum deflection angle at hit fraction f ≈ 0.7–0.9.
- **No experimental validation of any 15-ball break spread pattern; no systematic quantitative comparison of "sequential pairwise impulses vs simultaneous multibody integration"**—the only crossover point is the directional falsification in 1.3, and its direction is "the impulse method falsifies the smooth method". Note: the "2022 follow-up 2000fps validation" mentioned in the task prompt is the third experiment of this paper; no separate follow-up billiard validation paper by the group exists (the group has a 2020 precursor conference paper Corral, Gismeros et al., "Dynamic modeling and analysis of pool balls interaction", Comput. Methods Appl. Sci. vol 53, pp 79–86; and a 2021 contact review).

### 2.3 resolve_continually_touching (10% momentum-theft hack) measured
The code is at pooltool/physics/resolve/ball_ball/core.py:133, `theft_fraction = 0.10` at core.py:188; it runs on every ball-ball resolve (core.py:240): when the two balls' relative velocity along the line of centers is <1 mm/s and the cosine similarity of their velocity directions is >0.9, the chased ball "steals" 10% of the chaser's radial momentum. The docstring (core.py:171-174) claims "practically speaking, this is a no-op method for all but the most contrived simulation conditions". **Measured, this does not hold in the break**: monkeypatch counts show that 5 of the 20 runs at sf ≤ 1e-3 (25%) have a Newton's-cradle-style event explosion (ball-ball events rise from the normal ~40 to 333–354), in which the hack actually rewrites velocities 288–313 times/break; at sf ≥ 1e-2 it fires ≤1 time in total over 20 breaks. That is, **the tighter the rack, the more the sequential impulse method depends on this theoretically unfounded phenomenological hack to keep the simulation moving forward**, and the subsequent dynamics of these breaks are substantially shaped by ~290 10% momentum transfers.

### 2.4 Interpreting the spread-pattern sensitivity
- **Insensitive at the distribution level**: the between-group differences in pocketing rate, mean spread distance and total path across three orders of magnitude of sf (1e-4→1e-2) are all smaller than the within-group seed variance (n=10, pocketing rate 0.6→0.9 is about 1 standard error). Only at sf=1e-1 (nominal gap ~5.7 mm, already a clearly loose rack) does a discernible trend start to appear (pocketing rate rises to 1.1, corner-ball angle sd grows by an order of magnitude).
- **Extremely sensitive at the single-break level**: pure chaos, even 1 µm can change the pocketing outcome. So "reproducing one specific real break shot" is impossible for any scheme; only distribution matching is meaningful.
- **The first-order structure of the spread is locked by the lattice geometry**: the exit angles of the two back-row corner balls are pinned at 60.0°/120.0° (±0.02–0.2°) at all sf ≤ 1e-2—sequential impulses transmit momentum along the line of centers, so the direction is set by the rack geometry and is essentially independent of the spacing value; this agrees with the first-order physics of a Hertz chain (force along the line of centers), so the two approaches will not diverge much in direction, **the divergence is mainly in how energy/velocity is distributed among the balls** (the falsification case in the paper's 1.3 is exactly the distribution/direction going wrong at an ill-conditioned gap).

## 3. Conclusions

### (a) Conclusion on the sequential impulse scheme's sensitivity to spacing
The spread pattern is **not** "tuned in" by the spacing_factor value: within 1e-4–1e-2 (gap 5.7 µm–0.57 mm, covering the tight/loose range of real racks), the distribution statistics are robust to sf; what spacing really determines is (i) the specific realization of a single break (pure chaos seed) and (ii) whether it falls into the event-explosion ill-conditioned zone (25% probability of triggering the momentum-theft hack at sf ≤ 1e-3). Therefore **there is no need to fine-tune the spacing value with real footage**, but we recommend: sample with a seeded RNG from a distribution of a reasonable order of magnitude (recommended nominal gap 0.05–0.5 mm, i.e. sf ~ 1e-3–1e-2); if statistical fidelity is the goal, the **distribution metrics** of real break footage (pocketing rate, spread-radius histogram, ball-speed decay) can be used for a one-time check rather than per-parameter calibration. The real fidelity risk point is not the spacing value but the hack dependence under a tight rack (see 2.3).

### (b) Evaluation of the hybrid architecture (event-driven normally, switching to local compliant integration for the first few milliseconds of the break)
- **Computational cost: fully feasible**. Under Hertz theory the billiard ball-ball contact duration is ~100–300 µs (scaling as v^(-1/5)); taking dt = 1–2 µs and integrating the 2–5 ms contact window after the break, ~10³–10⁴ steps × 16 balls ~30 contact pairs, a compiled implementation's wall time is <10 ms, negligible compared with the event-driven main loop's ~hundreds of ms. The paper's hour-scale cost comes from Matlab full-duration (3–4 s) integration + a constraint solver, and does not represent the cost of a local hybrid scheme.
- **Determinism cost: low**. fixed-step + a fixed contact traversal order are enough for single-platform bit-reproducible results; cross-platform floating-point determinism issues (FMA, libm) are on the same level as those the event-driven kernel itself faces, adding no essential difficulty. It even has one fewer RNG dependency than "random spacing" (but rack placement itself still needs a seeded perturbation, otherwise a perfect lattice is a measure-zero non-physical input).
- **Implementation complexity: medium**. Needed: frozen-cluster detection at collision time (contact graph); a local compliant integrator (LN/Hertz force + contact-phase friction, velocity-Verlet or RK4); switch-back logic (exit condition: all δ<0 and relative velocities separating, handing (r,v,ω) back to the event queue). The parameter burden is the real cost: K (derivable as ~3.5×10⁸ N/m^1.5 from E=3800 MPa, ν=0.375), c_r=0.93 and the contact-phase friction model all need calibration, and the paper shows that the smooth method itself is sensitive to the initial gap (valid range only 10⁻⁹–10⁻⁷ m) and step size, and with improper parameters gives qualitative errors that the impulse method falsifies—**compliant is not a fidelity silver bullet, it only swaps the hack for parameters with physical meaning**.
- **Benefit**: eliminates two hacks (the dynamical role of spacing, 10% momentum-theft); force propagation in simultaneous multibody contact (Newton's cradle-type scenarios) is physically correct, and the energy distribution is grounded.

### (c) Final recommendation for our in-house engine
1. **Baseline: pooltool-style sequential impulses + tiny random gaps, the best cost-benefit solution**: the spread pattern is robust to spacing at the distribution level (40 local breaks measured), determinism is good (same seed reproduces bitwise), <300 ms per break. Use a seeded distribution for spacing with a nominal gap of 0.05–0.5 mm (physically corresponding to the tightness of a real rack), avoiding the ≤ 1e-4·R ill-conditioned zone.
2. **Do not copy resolve_continually_touching's 10% momentum-theft**: in tight-rack breaks it is not an edge case (fires in 25% of breaks, ~290 times per break). Alternatives in increasing complexity: (i) on detecting a frozen chain, iterate sequential impulses to convergence (Newton's cradle is solvable); (ii) a small-scale LCP/impulse-propagation solve of the cluster; (iii) i.e. the hybrid architecture's local compliant integration—enabled only for events where "the same-instant contact graph has a connected component of >2 balls".
3. **The hybrid architecture as an optional phase-2 enhancement (feature flag)**, motivated by removing the hack and correct multibody force propagation, not by a "more accurate spread pattern"—the two approaches are hard to tell apart on the spread distribution within the calibratable range, and the academic side (Gismeros Moreno et al. 2022) has no 15-ball spread-pattern experimental validation to back it either; to really claim fidelity, the only path is to capture our own high-speed footage for a distribution-level check, which costs the same for both approaches.
4. If doing the hybrid: K ≈ 3.5×10⁸ N/m^1.5, c_r = 0.93, n = 1.5, Alciatore μ(v) (pooltool's default friction comes from the same source as the paper's, so they can be compared directly), dt ≤ 2 µs, integration window ≤ 5 ms, fixed-step for determinism; use the event-driven version as the regression oracle (a large directional deviation raises an alarm, echoing the paper's precedent of the impulse method falsifying the smooth method).
