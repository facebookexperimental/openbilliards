# literature

## KEY TAKEAWAYS
- A realistic billiards simulation must build its own event-based physics kernel: analytic equations of motion + polynomial root finding to predict events (Leckie & Greenspan 2006, pooltool JOSS 2024); the micro-timestep rigid-body approach of general-purpose game engines falls short in both accuracy and feel.
- The feel watershed four-pack: throw produced by velocity-dependent ball-ball friction (at most about 5°), squirt from the endmass model, a cushion model with a velocity-dependent effective coefficient of restitution, and cloth friction with three independent coefficients (sliding/rolling/spinning decay independently).
- Ball-cloth parameters have first-hand measurements: sliding μs≈0.2 (snooker measured 0.178-0.245, varying with slip speed), rolling μr≈0.01 (measured 0.0127-0.0129), spin deceleration 5-15 rad/s²; the sliding-phase trajectory is a parabola, and massé/swerve emerges naturally from it (Coriolis 1835).
- Ball-ball contact lasts only 100-600 μs, e=0.92-0.98, so the instantaneous-impulse approximation holds; but ball-ball friction that decays with relative slip speed, μ(v)=0.009951+0.108e^(-1.088v) (Alciatore TP A-14 fit, pooltool default), must be added to reproduce CIT/SIT and spin transfer.
- Best cushion model: the Han 2005 analytic model for everyday use (note that the pooltool author corrected two formula errors in the original), Mathavan 2010 for calibration—contact height h=7R/5 (0.7 ball diameters, matching the WPA 63.5% spec), ee=0.98, μw=0.14, the rigid-cushion assumption holds only for normal speed <2.5 m/s, and the equivalent COR of a rolling head-on impact is 0.818 and falls with speed along a quadratic curve.
- Strike contact about 1-2 ms; squirt is determined by the effective mass of the cue end (Mb/Mtip≈20-100 → squirt about 2.33°-0.49° @ b/R=3/8); a rigid whole-cue model overestimates it up to 16°, so the endmass (~12 g) model is required; the miscue limit is an offset of 0.5R (Coriolis theory 0.7R).
- Pocket mouths must use the real WPA geometry (corner pocket 142°±1°, side pocket 104°±1° cut angle, back-draft 12-15°, shelf 0-2.25 inches); treat the pocket points as cushion segments reusing the cushion model so that rattle emerges; the effective pocket size changes dynamically with speed/incidence angle.
- In the break, 15 frozen balls make instantaneous pairwise impulses fail: the engineering mainstream is tiny random gaps (pooltool spacing_factor defaults to 0.001 and setting it to 0 is not recommended), the academic approach is short-time integration of nonlinear smooth contact forces (Gismeros Moreno et al., Nonlinear Dynamics 2021).
- Jump shots/landing: a legal jump shot is a downward stroke after which the ball rebounds off the slate (scoop is a foul), ball-bed COR 0.5-0.7; the airborne phase is pure projectile motion, and on landing a sliding-friction impulse redistributes translation and rotation.
- The most important public assets: Dr. Dave's (Alciatore) Technical Proofs and table of measured constants, the Loughborough Mathavan series of high-speed-video papers, and the open-source implementation pooltool that can be compared against directly—we recommend using the TP throw curves and the Mathavan rebound curves as regression test baselines for the physics kernel.

## OPEN QUESTIONS
- No first-hand material at all was found on the internal physics implementation of commercial simulators (closed source); 'commercial-reference-grade feel = throw+squirt+cushion velocity dependence' is an engineering inference based on literature importance and player perception, not officially confirmed.
- The original Dr. Dave TP A-14 PDF is image-typeset and cannot be extracted directly; the constants of μ(v)=0.009951+0.108e^(-1.088v) are cited indirectly via the pooltool documentation (AlciatoreBallBallFriction) and were not checked word for word against the TP original.
- The coefficient of restitution/friction values used in the Han 2005 original were not read directly (koreascience has only the abstract page); the model structure and the basis for the 'two formula errors' are the pooltool author's reproduction and correction documents.
- Cross 2020 "Impact force between two colliding billiard balls" is paywalled in full; the specific measured values of ball-ball contact time and peak force are unverified (only the method description in the abstract is available).
- No systematic first-hand measurement was found of whether the ball-ball coefficient of restitution varies significantly with speed (the internal structure of the 0.92-0.98 range).
- The content of Marlow's book is all second-hand citation (Dr. Dave resource page, Mathavan papers); the original book was not consulted directly; its rail COR 0.55 and rolling friction measurements have been questioned by Mathavan 2009.
- Effect of the cloth nap direction (nap) on ball paths: Williams claims it exists, Mathavan 2009 found no evidence; unresolved (British snooker cloth may still need a modelling toggle).
- Quantitative literature on in-pocket/pocket-point 3D geometry (the ball hitting the shelf and bouncing back onto the bed) is almost nonexistent; there are only the WPA geometry specs and the behavior-level experiments of Dr. Dave/Jewett, so our own kernel will need its own calibration.

## REPORT
# Billiards physics literature review: the list of phenomena a realistic billiards simulator must reproduce, and the best known models

## 0. Executive summary

Billiards physics has a clear main line in the literature: Coriolis (1835, "Théorie mathématique des effets du jeu de billard") → Marlow (1995, "The Physics of Pocket Billiards") → Alciatore (Dr. Dave, Colorado State, Technical Proofs + high-speed video from 1999 to the present) → the Loughborough Mathavan/Jackson/Parkin series (2009-2015, ball-cloth, ball-cushion and ball-ball models calibrated with high-speed video) → Han 2005 (analytic carom cushion model) → the open-source simulator pooltool (Kiefl, JOSS 2024), which engineered these models and corrected errors in the original papers. **Conclusion: a simulator with "commercial-reference-grade feel" cannot use a general-purpose game physics engine (instantaneous rigid bodies + constant coefficient of restitution); it must build its own event-based kernel and implement at least: velocity-dependent ball-ball friction (throw), squirt from the endmass model, a Mathavan/Han-grade cushion model (velocity-dependent effective coefficient of restitution + spin coupling), and cloth friction with three independent coefficients (sliding/rolling/spinning).** These are exactly the watershed phenomena that high-speed video in the literature has repeatedly verified and that players can perceive directly.

## 2. Per-phenomenon details and sources

### 2.1 Ball-cloth (phenomenon 1)
A ball on the cloth has 4+1 motion states: stationary, spinning in place, sliding, rolling, airborne, each with analytic equations of motion (the trajectory is quadratic in time; while sliding, the friction direction along the contact point's relative velocity û is constant, so the sliding-phase trajectory is a parabola—exactly the result already known in Coriolis's time, and also the source of swerve/massé curves). The three friction coefficients **must be independent**:
- Sliding μs: Dr. Dave constant table 0.15-0.4 (typical 0.2) https://drdavepoolinfo.com/faq/physics/physical-properties/; Mathavan et al. measured snooker cloth at 0.178-0.245 (mean 0.212) with high-speed video, and it **varies with sliding speed** (AJP 2009, https://drdavepoolinfo.com/physics_articles/ajp_09_hsv_article.pdf).
- Rolling μr: snooker measured deceleration 0.124-0.126 m/s² (μr≈0.0128, constant, independent of speed, arising from cloth deformation); Marlow's book gives pool cloth 0.011-0.024, mean 0.016—the same paper also points out that Marlow's "meter stick + stopwatch" method has large errors, and **found no evidence of an effect of the cloth nap direction (nap)**.
- Spin μsp: independent linear decay of vertical-axis spin ωz(t)=ω0z−(5μsp·g/2R)t; real point contact in theory produces no spin torque, so μsp is a phenomenological parameter (the pooltool theory page says outright that this is "cheating", https://ekiefl.github.io/2020/04/24/pooltool-theory/). Dr. Dave gives a spin deceleration of 5-15 rad/s².
Classic textbook: Marlow "The Physics of Pocket Billiards" (1995, ISBN 9780964537002, https://www.abebooks.com/9780964537002/Physics-Pocket-Billiards-Wayland-Marlow-0964537001/plp); but note that Kiefl and Mathavan both point out that Marlow's treatment of the cushion is "incomplete and inconsistent".

### 2.2 Ball-ball collision (phenomenon 2)
- Contact time: Marlow measured 100-600 μs, Sorokin measured 250-300 μs, shorter at higher speed (https://drdavepoolinfo.com/faq/ball/contact-time/) → the instantaneous-impulse approximation fully holds for a single-pair collision. Cross 2020 (Physics Education 55, 065002, https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6552/aba207) estimated the contact force with a spring model + rigid-surface experiments (full text paywalled, values unchecked).
- Coefficient of restitution: e=0.92-0.98 (Dr. Dave constant table).
- **Throw (collision-induced deflection) and spin transfer**: ball-ball friction μ=0.03-0.08, decaying exponentially with the relative slip speed at the contact point. pooltool's default AlciatoreBallBallFriction (a=0.009951, b=0.108, c=1.088) is the fit from Dr. Dave TP A-14 (https://pooltool.readthedocs.io/en/latest/resources/custom_physics.html). Behavioral points (https://drdavepoolinfo.com/faq/throw/maximum/): maximum throw ≈5° (about 1 inch of deviation per foot); CIT is largest at slow speed + stun + a half-ball hit; SIT is largest at slow speed + stun + about 50% sidespin; at cut angles <20° speed has almost no effect on CIT; both follow and draw reduce throw; outside english at the gearing ratio brings throw to zero. Full numerical model: Mathavan, Jackson, Parkin, "Numerical simulations of the frictional collisions of solid balls on a rough surface", Sports Engineering 2014 (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12283-014-0158-y), i.e. pooltool's frictional_mathavan (default, 1000 iteration steps). Dr. Dave's TP index (TP A-14, A-17, A-26, A-27, A-28, B-31, etc., https://drdavepoolinfo.com/technical-proof/) provides all derivations and calibration curves.
- Recent additions: Hyeong-Chan Kim's 2024 paper on ball-ball collisions with spin and 2021/2022 papers on post-strike motion (listed on the Dr. Dave resource page, https://drdavepoolinfo.com/physics/).

### 2.3 Ball-cushion (phenomenon 3)
Two complementary models:
- **Han 2005** "Dynamics in carom and three cushion billiards" (J. Mech. Sci. Tech. 19(4), https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO200531935952675.pub): an analytic model with instantaneous impulse, a rigid cushion and stick/slide branches; low computational cost; pooltool's default cushion model (han_2005). **Note: while reproducing it, Kiefl found and corrected two formula errors in Han's original** (corrected derivation: https://ekiefl.github.io/images/pooltool/pooltool-theory/ball_cushion_inhwan_han.pdf).
- **Mathavan/Jackson/Parkin 2010** "A theoretical analysis of billiard ball dynamics under cushion impacts" (Proc. IMechE Part C 224, https://drdavepoolinfo.com/physics_articles/Mathavan_IMechE_2010.pdf): cushion nose contact height h=7R/5 (common to snooker and pool; i.e. 0.7 ball diameters, consistent with the WPA 63.5%±1% spec), sinθ=2/5; numerical integration with impulse as the independent variable (Stronge energy coefficient of restitution framework); fitting the experiments gives **ee=0.98, μw=0.14**; **the rigid-cushion assumption is valid only for normal incoming speed <2.5 m/s**; the equivalent COR of a rolling ball's head-on impact =0.818 (including friction and 3D impulse effects), and rebound vs incoming speed is a quadratic curve (y=-0.0877x²+1.131x-0.0953, 0.28-3.5 m/s)—i.e. **the effective coefficient of restitution falls with speed**, which is the first-hand experimental basis for "cushion velocity dependence"; with heavy sidespin the rebound speed can exceed the incoming speed (spin energy injection).
- Cushion nose height effect (perceptible to players): too high → slow rebound, longer banks; too low → fast rebound, shorter banks, jumping balls (https://drdavepoolinfo.com/faq/table/cushion-nose-height/). Marlow's rail COR 0.55 is questioned by Mathavan 2009. TP 6.3/7.3 treat how the bank angle varies with COR and topspin/backspin.
- Ball frozen to the cushion (ball frozen to cushion), in simultaneous contact with the bed: the Mathavan 2010 model explicitly contains two impulses, at the bed contact point C and the cushion contact point I (PIx/PIy and PCx/PCy), and is the correct reference for this problem; the Han model lacks this term.

### 2.4 Strike (phenomenon 4)
- Contact duration: Shepard gives ~1 ms; Cross 2008 measured a typical impact at 2.1 ms with piezoelectric sensors, sliding lengthens the contact to 1-2 ms, an aluminium-rod cue 3.0 ms (http://www.physics.usyd.edu.au/~cross/PUBLICATIONS/39.%20squirt.pdf).
- **Squirt/endmass**: Ron Shepard 2001 "Everything You Always Wanted to Know About Cue Ball Squirt" (https://drdavepoolinfo.com/physics_articles/Shepard_squirt.pdf): conservation of lateral momentum Mtip·Vtip,y + Mb·Vb,y = 0, **only the Mb/Mtip ratio matters**; at b/R=3/8, Mb/Mtip=20→ψ=2.33° (pivot 10.28"), Mb/Mtip=100→ψ=0.49° (pivot 49.11"); real cues Mb/Mtip≈20-100, so real cues differ in squirt by about 4-5 times; within the practical range squirt is approximately linear in b/R. Cross 2008 (Am. J. Phys. 76(3), 205-212) confirmed experimentally: after chalking, the tip "grips" the ball throughout and slips only once F/N rises to about 0.7; near a miscue squirt can reach 6°. Kim 2021 (arXiv:2104.11232, https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11232) gives a complete analytic treatment based on Stronge impulse dynamics: the slip phase is the physical origin of squirt; **a rigid whole-cue model overestimates the squirt at a half-radius offset to ~16°; a flexible/endmass model in which only the cue end (~12 g, ~12 cm) takes part must be used**—this also explains low-deflection carbon-fiber cues. Engineering conclusion: squirt can be tuned with the single endmass parameter, and pooltool's instantaneous_point model even exposes squirt_throttle/english_throttle knobs directly.
- **Miscue limit**: maximum effective offset = 0.5R (beyond it is a miscue; the stripe width of a striped ball, exactly 0.5D, can serve as a reference) (http://drdavepoolinfo.com/faq/sidespin/maximum/); Coriolis theoretical limit 0.7R (cited by Kim 2021).
- **Jump shot**: legal jump shot = elevated cue stroking downward; the ball is pressed into the bed and then rebounds off the slate to take off; scooping (scoop) is a foul (https://drdavepoolinfo.com/faq/jump/).
- **Massé**: Coriolis 1835 (Nadler 2005 English translation, source given on the Dr. Dave resource page)—the sliding friction direction is constant → the curve is a parabola; Coriolis/BAR aiming system: "any aim point on the RA line in theory gives the same final direction angle" (https://drdavepoolinfo.com/faq/masse/aiming/, TP A-19). In engineering terms, if both the cloth sliding friction and the initial (v,ω) of the strike are modelled correctly, massé/swerve emerges naturally, with no dedicated model needed.

### 2.5 Airborne flight and landing (phenomenon 5)
The airborne phase is pure projectile motion (air drag could be added, but the literature generally ignores it); landing uses the ball-bed coefficient of restitution e=0.5-0.7 (slate cushioned by the cloth, Dr. Dave constant table), and at the instant of the bounce a sliding-friction impulse redistributes translation/rotation. pooltool's architectural convention is worth borrowing: ball-ball/ball-cushion events do not themselves put the ball into the airborne state; instead the ball-slate event that immediately follows (quadratic equation root finding) handles it uniformly (https://ekiefl.github.io/2020/12/20/pooltool-alg/). For modelling the bounce of a spinning hard ball on a compliant surface, see arXiv:2208.11685 (analysis of point-contact bounce models).

### 2.6 Pocket mouth (phenomenon 6)
The first-hand geometry source is the WPA equipment specification (https://www.cuesight.com/wpa/tournament-table-equipment-specifications/): corner pocket mouth 4.5-4.625", side pocket mouth 5-5.125", horizontal cut angle 142°±1° (corner)/104°±1° (side), vertical back-draft 12°-15°, shelf depth corner pocket 1-2.25"/side pocket 0-0.375". At the behavior level (https://drdavepoolinfo.com/faq/pocket/size-and-center/, TP 3.5-3.12): **both the effective pocket size and the "pocket center" change dynamically with ball speed and incidence angle**—a slow ball at a shallow angle can brush the cushion first and then roll in (effective pocket greatly enlarged), while for a fast ball it shrinks markedly; the ideal aim-point offset can reach 0.6" (corner pocket)/0.3" (side pocket). The correct engineering approach to rattle is not to write dedicated logic but to build the pocket points (jaw) as cushion segments with real geometry: pooltool connects line + arc cushion segments smoothly and reuses the Han model, so rattle emerges naturally; its default ball-pocket test is still "circular trigger + removal", which the author's own note flags as a rough spot—our own kernel should switch to a real-geometry test (ball center past the pocket mouth edge + below the lip height h≈7R/5).

### 2.7 Break: simultaneous multi-ball collisions (phenomenon 7)
With 15 frozen balls the instantaneous pairwise impulse model fails (the Newton's cradle problem: the result depends on processing order, and the energy/momentum distribution is not unique). Treatments in the literature and in engineering:
1. **Tiny gaps + sequential events** (the mainstream game approach): pooltool's generate_layout/get_rack take a spacing_factor (default 0.001, i.e. random gaps on the order of one thousandth of the ball radius), and the documentation states explicitly "setting it to 0 is not recommended" (https://pooltool.readthedocs.io/en/latest/autoapi/pooltool/layouts/index.html). Physical justification: real racks are never completely frozen either.
2. **Smooth (compliant) contact force models**: Gismeros Moreno, Corral Abad, Meneses Alonso, Gómez García, Castejón Sisamón, "Modelling multiple-simultaneous impact problems with a nonlinear smooth approach: pool/billiard application", Nonlinear Dynamics 2021 (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11071-021-07117-4, cited 23 times): short-time numerical integration of the whole break with nonlinear contact forces (Hertz/Hunt-Crossley family) + friction, a systematic comparison of the accuracy/cost of the contact force models and solvers, with experimental validation; it explicitly points out pitfalls of the smooth approach under simultaneous multiple contacts, such as "detecting contact onset".
3. If discrete time is insisted on: keeping penetration within R/100 at 10 m/s needs a ~30 μs step (pooltool-alg blog post). Recommended hybrid architecture: event-based normally, switching locally to compliant contact integration at the moment of the break.

### 2.8 Recent literature and academic simulators (phenomenon 8)
- **Mathavan series**: AJP 2009 high-speed video methodology (above); IMechE 2010 cushion; Sports Engineering 2014 numerical model of ball-ball frictional collisions; 2015 IEEE/ASME Trans. Mechatronics snooker robot (http://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/31391/1/PubSub8899_Mathavan.pdf); the 2009 PhD thesis is listed on the Dr. Dave resource page.
- **Simulator lineage**: Leckie & Greenspan "An Event-Based Pool Physics Simulator" (Advances in Computer Games 2006, https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/11922155_19); PoolFiz/FastFiz belong to this branch (used for the computational billiards tournaments, Archibald et al. 2009); the best active open-source implementation is **pooltool** (Kiefl, JOSS 9(101):7301, 2024, https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.07301; long-form theory and algorithm posts https://ekiefl.github.io/2020/04/24/pooltool-theory/ and https://ekiefl.github.io/2020/12/20/pooltool-alg/): event-based, pluggable models (ball-ball: frictionless_elastic / frictional_inelastic / frictional_mathavan; cushion: han_2005; strike: instantaneous_point), the best architectural reference and comparison implementation for our own kernel.
- **AI/data direction**: Billiards Sports Analytics dataset and task survey (arXiv:2407.19686); CueTip physics-aware billiards assistant (arXiv:2501.18291); Learning Visual Predictive Models of Physics for Playing Billiards (arXiv:1511.07404).
- **Review**: Strafford, Bell & Stone "Snooker Science: A Cross-Discipline Scoping Review", Communications in Kinesiology 2025 (https://shura.shu.ac.uk/36336/): of 261 search hits only 14 papers were included, confirming that the academic billiards literature is sparse overall—the first-hand literature a physics kernel can rely on is exactly the batch above.
- Kim (Korea Nat'l Univ. of Transportation) 2021-2024 analytic series on the strike and on collisions with spin (arXiv:2104.11232 etc.).

## 3. Engineering recommendations (by priority)

1. **Architecture**: adopt a Leckie-Greenspan/pooltool-style event-based kernel (analytic evolution + polynomial root-finding events), with floating-point-level accuracy and performance far beyond micro-timestep integration; the break goes separately through compliant-contact numerical integration or the spacing_factor approach.
2. **The feel watershed four-pack** (the minimal sufficient set for commercial-reference-grade realism, all calibratable with first-hand experimental data): velocity-dependent ball-ball friction → throw/spin transfer (Alciatore μ(v) fit or Mathavan 2014); endmass squirt + swerve produced by the analytic cloth sliding solution (Shepard/Cross/Kim); Han 2005 (corrected version) cushion + the Mathavan 2010 velocity-dependent effective COR curve (its quadratic fit can be used directly as a lookup table); cloth friction with three independent coefficients and independent sidespin decay.
3. **Pocket mouths use real geometry** (WPA spec + jaw as cushion segments), no circular triggers; rattle must be emergent behavior.
4. **All parameters tunable, with literature defaults** (see the cross-reference table); use Dr. Dave's TP curves (throw vs cut angle/speed/spin) and Mathavan's rebound curves as regression test baselines.
