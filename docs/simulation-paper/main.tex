\title{OpenBilliards: a validated, bit-exact event-driven simulator for
cue-sports mechanics, with a systematic assessment of the empirical laws of
pool}

\author{OpenBilliards contributors}

\begin{abstract}
To our knowledge, this paper presents the first systematic
domains-of-validity assessment of the empirical laws of pool---the
$30^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ rules, the throw, squirt, and swerve laws,
gearing english, the ten-times-fuller and trisect systems, and the
bank/kick diamond systems. The instrument is OpenBilliards, a validated
event-driven analytic rigid-body simulator for cue-sports mechanics
whose shipped WebAssembly binary is bit-exact across tested platforms at
sub-millisecond typical per-shot cost and serves as the paper's
normative physics definition. A battery of 24
experiments, internally registered as frozen designs before any sweep
ran,
executed 16{,}933 recorded simulations, distilled into 25 byte-stable
pinned outputs that include a cross-simulator oracle run against the
open simulator pooltool. Results are reported in ASME V\&V~20
vocabulary---comparison error $E = S - D$ with validation uncertainty
$u_{\mathrm{val}}$---with every anchor classified as measured datum,
derived reference form, or practitioner lore (``validated'' claims rest
on the measured class), and every registered-band miss is recorded in a
machine-readable waiver ledger (38 rows, seven of them registered-band
misses) rather than silently re-tuned.
Two headline findings illustrate the method. The trisect ``$3\times$
cut'' rule is exact only at its half-ball calibration point: the
measured ratio declines from 3.99 to 2.59 over $5$--$45^\circ$ cuts and
stays within 10\% of 3 only for $22.5$--$40^\circ$ cuts. The battery
also uncovered two inherited defects in our own kernel---a ball--ball
slip-impulse law leaving slip-regime throw about 2.6\% low, and a
discontinuous cushion rebound-branch selection shifting bank arrivals
by up to 0.85 diamonds---each fixed and confirmed by predicted-shift
verification. Every figure regenerates from pinned outputs whose physics
is proven identical to the shipped canonical binary: all 16{,}933
recorded simulations replay through that binary with stamp-normalized
trajectory-digest equality (16{,}933/16{,}933), and successful
reproduction is defined by hash equality. The kernel source is public
under the MIT license, with the portions derived from pooltool under the
Apache License~2.0; the canonical kernel revision of this paper, the
experiment harness, and the pinned outputs are not part of that release.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Supplementary material}
\label{sec:supplementary}

Supplementary material associated with this article is distributed as a
separate Supplementary Material document, built from the same sources
(sections numbered S1--S6).

\emph{Section~S1 (data provenance)} maps every figure and table of this
paper to its experiment ID, pinned output hash (SHA-256 prefix), and
registered source, and derives the abstract's 16{,}933-simulation count
from the replay corpus.

\emph{Section~S2 (tier-1 reproduction mechanics)} states the artifact
bundle inventory, the five-step reproduction procedure, the
physics-identity checker semantics, the reproducibility caveats, and the
licensing terms summarized in Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}.

\emph{Section~S3 (governing equations and frozen constants)} reproduces
the complete governing equations of every model-catalog row of
Table~\ref{tab:models}, with each source's deviations itemized and every
frozen constant consolidated with units and provenance.

\emph{Section~S4 (extended results, groups B and D)} carries the
per-experiment result prose for the stick--ball and curved-path groups,
whose summary tables and non-obvious findings remain in
Sections~\ref{sec:results-b} and~\ref{sec:results-d}.

\emph{Section~S5 (predicted-shift verification)} transcribes the full
seven-class predicted-shift table behind the two-row extract of
Section~\ref{sec:oracle} (Table~\ref{tab:predicted-shifts}).

\emph{Section~S6 (video-artifact presentation methods)} documents the
deterministic time-remap and shutter synthesis behind the artifact
bundle's validated-scenario video pack
(Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}); the videos present
already-verified kernel records, and no claim of this paper rests on
them.
