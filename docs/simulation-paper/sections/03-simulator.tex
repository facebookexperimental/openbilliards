\section{The simulator}
\label{sec:simulator}

The instrument under study is an event-driven analytic rigid-body simulator
for cue-sports mechanics. Between discrete events, every ball state evolves
in closed form; events are detected by root-finding on low-degree
polynomials and resolved by a catalog of impact models, each carrying an
explicit published provenance and a frozen parameter set. The kernel ships
as a single WebAssembly binary that this paper treats as the normative
definition of the physics (Section~\ref{sec:simulator-determinism}).
Consistent with that posture, this section describes the simulator through
its model provenance and its verifiable outputs rather than through source
code (public at a later revision; Section~\ref{sec:availability});
Fig.~\ref{fig:F1} summarizes the architecture.

\subsection{Event-driven analytic core}
\label{sec:simulator-core}

The formulation descends from the event-based pool physics of
\citet{leckie2006event}: rather than time-stepping, the kernel advances
each ball along a closed-form trajectory determined by its current motion
regime and solves for the earliest time at which any regime transition or
contact occurs. Cloth interaction is modeled with three independent
friction regimes---sliding, rolling, and spin about the vertical
axis---each with its own coefficient, so that a ball's planar trajectory
within a regime is exactly quadratic in time and airborne flight is exactly
ballistic. The design deliberately excludes air drag and Magnus lift:
admitting either would destroy the polynomial character of the
trajectories, and with it the guarantee that every event time is a root of
a polynomial of degree at most four (the degree-four case arising from
ball--ball proximity between two quadratic trajectories).

Quartic event detection uses the closed-form solver of
\citet{orellana2020algorithm1010}, incorporating both published corrections
\citep{demichele2022remark,demichele2024remark}. \citet{leckie2006event}
already flagged root-finding fragility as the Achilles' heel of
event-driven billiards simulation; the kernel's posture at the residual
hard cases---structurally coincident roots at grazing and stick-onset
configurations---is a deterministic first-crossing selection rule, discussed
as a robustness property in Section~\ref{sec:simulator-robustness} and, as
a case study of a defect this posture replaced, in
Section~\ref{sec:oracle}. Each resolved event then dispatches to an impact
model from the catalog of Section~\ref{sec:simulator-catalog}, and the
resulting event stream---not any sampled rendering of it---is the
simulator's output of record.

\subsection{Model catalog with provenance}
\label{sec:simulator-catalog}

Table~\ref{tab:models} lists the physical channels, the model identifiers
under which they are recorded in every battery output, their published
sources, and their frozen parameters. The complete governing equations of
every catalog row, with each source's deviations itemized and the frozen
constants consolidated, are reproduced in Supplementary Material
Section~S3. Two doctrinal points govern the
catalog. First, a \emph{parameter freeze}: catalog values were frozen
before any battery sweep ran, and every subsequent miss became a ledger row
rather than a re-tune (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}). The worked example
is the declared ball--ball restitution $e_b = 0.95$, which differs from the
$e = 1$ idealization used by several reference derivations and produces a
coherent, declared family of comparison errors across the battery,
reported as declared model content, not error (quantified per experiment in
Section~\ref{sec:results}). Second, \emph{provenance honesty}: where the
sources themselves drift, both readings are recorded---the kernel's
ball--ball friction curve $\mu(v_{\mathrm{rel}}) = 0.009951 + 0.108\,
e^{-1.088\,v_{\mathrm{rel}}}$ follows the current revision of the
velocity-dependent throw fit in Alciatore's technical-proof corpus
\citep{alciatore_tp_a14}, while an earlier document in the same corpus
prints a different decay constant; the discrepancy is ledgered as a
source revision drift rather than silently harmonized.

The ball--ball slip-friction impulse uses the Coulomb-cap law (slip
impulse $\mu\,(1+e_b)/2\cdot v_n$, recorded in outputs as
\code{CoulombCap}). This law is itself an outcome of the paper's
methodology: the battery surfaced an inherited mis-scaled convention
(the slip-law defect), the corrected law became the default, and the
legacy law is
retained behind an explicit option with byte-identical replay proofs
(case study in Section~\ref{sec:oracle}).

Cushion impact offers one default and three alternative models: the
corrected Han carom impulse model as validation default
\citep{han2005dynamics}, a compliant Stronge energetic-restitution
alternative \citep{stronge2018impact}, and two fitted equipment presets
carrying velocity-dependent restitution responses---the $e_c(v)$ hook
that couples rebound speed and spin to approach conditions.
Identifiers, frozen parameters, and---importantly---each preset's
\emph{declared validity domain} are the corresponding rows of
Table~\ref{tab:models}; the fitted presets are out-of-domain on several
US-pool bank and lag observables (quantified in
Section~\ref{sec:results}), and
neither was re-tuned.

Cushion \emph{geometry} deserves its own paragraph, because it is a
documented trap. The kernel uses the US-pool cushion-nose height of
63.5\% of ball diameter, per the WPA equipment specification;
\citet{mathavan2010cushion} place the cushion contact at $7R/5$---70\% of
ball diameter---which is correct for the snooker equipment their model was
fit on and systematically skews US-pool bank angles if transplanted. We
treat this as a domain separation between equipment classes, not a
disagreement with the source; the fitted Mathavan preset accordingly pairs
with the snooker table configuration, and the bank experiment sweeps nose
height over a 0.60--0.67 sensitivity bracket around the specification
value to bound the geometric channel. The remaining rows of
Table~\ref{tab:models}---endmass squirt for stick--ball interaction
\citep{cross2008squirt,alciatore_tp_a31,shepard2001squirt} and the
three-coefficient cloth model verified against the closed-form kinematics
of the technical-proof corpus \citep{alciatore_tp_catalog}---are exercised
by experiment groups B and F respectively (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\subsection{Compliant frozen-cluster break resolution (C3)}
\label{sec:simulator-break}

The break shot confronts an event-driven kernel with its degenerate case.
A racked set of balls is a \emph{frozen-contact topology}: in the battery's
recorded rack, the apex-wedge pairs sit at a surface separation of
$0.5\,\mu$m---far below the $10\,\mu$m cluster-contact threshold---while
seeded micro-gaps appear only at back-row boundaries (the battery's seed-0
topology carries one open 0.16\,mm boundary between rows three and four,
truncating the connected cluster at the cue ball plus six balls).
Instantaneous pairwise impulse resolution is ill-posed on such clusters:
conservation laws alone underdetermine the outcome of a simultaneous
multi-ball impact \citep{cross2023oblique}. The failure mode is not
hypothetical. Decomposing the break into a serial chain of pairwise
impacts starves the multi-ball momentum exchange, and the battery's break
experiment explicitly gates on the absence of the resulting defect class,
in which the cue ball arrives at the rack and stops nearly dead (a settled
squat fraction below 0.05); no such row appears anywhere on the registered
grid under the shipped resolver.

The kernel therefore embeds a bounded compliant episode inside the
otherwise closed-form event stream. When an impact reaches a frozen
cluster, the cluster is advanced with Hertz contact stiffness and
Lankarani--Nikravesh hysteresis damping
\citep{lankarani1990contact,lankarani1994continuous}, all contacts
integrated concurrently in Jacobi fashion rather than sequentially, with
friction applied as force-based Coulomb traction during the episode and
restitution kept consistent with the energetic definition of
\citet{stronge2018impact}. The episode is strictly bounded---a $10^{-6}$\,s
internal step and at most 3000 steps, with the stiffness and damping
constants frozen in the catalog and recorded in each output's parameter
block---after which control returns to the analytic event loop. This
placement distinguishes the design from both neighboring approaches. Smooth
multibody treatments of pool impacts
\citep{corral2020poolballs,gismerosmoreno2022multiple} integrate the
\emph{entire} game with penalty time-stepping; we integrate only the
degenerate cluster episode, preserving closed-form determinism everywhere
else. At the other pole, pooltool documents a 10\% momentum-reduction
workaround for the break \citep{kiefl2024pooltool}; the battery realizes
that baseline class as the kernel's own \code{SequentialImpulse} legacy
arm---a pairwise resolver retained as an explicit option---so the rejected
alternative is runnable like-for-like rather than merely cited.

The model contrast is quantified by experiments \expid{E1} and \expid{E3}
(Figures~\ref{fig:F11} and \ref{fig:F12}; measured values in
Section~\ref{sec:results-e}): the compliant resolver reproduces the
ten-times-fuller kissed-ball slope and the qualitative effective-mass
scaling seen in high-speed break footage, both of which the sequential
arm erases---and the compliant rows survived the kernel's mid-program
re-baseline bit-identically, because force-based Coulomb traction
consumes no impulse cap. One compliant-side residual --- a speed-dependent
frozen-pair combination-throw excess over the measured anchor, with no
counterpart in the sequential arm --- remains open and is quantified
where it is measured (Sections~\ref{sec:results-e} and~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Determinism doctrine: the canonical WebAssembly binary}
\label{sec:simulator-determinism}

The kernel's determinism contract attaches to a specific artifact: the
shipped WebAssembly binary, version stamp \code{0.1.0+fb3425ad} (SHA-256
prefix \code{5e3b6ee4}; full hash and manifest in Supplementary Material
Section~S2), \emph{is} the physics definition of record, and native and
Python builds of the same kernel are declared development conveniences,
held to the binary by byte comparison where they are exercised but never
themselves normative. The doctrine has one home,
Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}: why WebAssembly can carry the
obligation, the stamp-lineage proof connecting the battery's native
build to the canonical binary, the replay contract, the determinism
audit matrix, and the measured platform sensitivity that makes the
binary --- not the source --- the right canonical artifact.

\subsection{Robustness: rest projection and event-detection continuity (C5)}
\label{sec:simulator-robustness}

Two engineering properties, sized deliberately below the headline claims,
close the loop between the analytic core and the determinism doctrine.
First, \emph{terminal rest projection}: every simulation ends by projecting
all balls to exact rest states, so that any \code{simulate} output is a
valid \code{simulate} input---the round-trip law. Combined with the no-NaN
invariant, this makes arbitrarily long shot chains (replays, rule
adjudication, and the battery's own multi-shot protocols) closed under the
API with no accumulating representational debt. Second, \emph{continuity
at structural double roots}: where an event polynomial's physical root is
structurally coincident---the configuration class
\citet{leckie2006event} identified as fragile---the kernel selects the
first crossing by a deterministic fixed-resolution scan rather than by
bracketing, which is continuous in the incidence parameters by
construction. The value of this posture is measurable: the legacy
bracketing selection produced the discontinuous bank arrivals of the
branch-selection defect, and under first-crossing selection the
discontinuous cell count fell
from four to zero with the divergence class measured empty on every pinned
artifact (case study in
Section~\ref{sec:oracle}).

\subsection{Computational performance}
\label{sec:simulator-performance}

Performance is an engineering property of the implementation, reported
here for context; nothing in the paper's verification or validation
claims depends on it. All timings in this subsection were measured on one
stated host --- an Apple M2~Ultra (macOS~26.5, 128\,GB), single-threaded
on both engines --- and are indicative rather than normative: unlike the
battery outputs, the performance artifact is hardware-pinned and carries
no expected hash.

Table~\ref{tab:perf} summarizes the two measurements. The first block
times the canonical WebAssembly binary of
Section~\ref{sec:simulator-determinism} (stamp \code{0.1.0+fb3425ad},
executed in Node.js through the same loader the application uses) on the
kernel's continuously-gated budget workloads: a typical two-ball shot
resolves in about 0.19\,ms at the median, a full 15-ball break in about
7.9\,ms, the heaviest seeded break workload --- a 22-ball snooker break
at its worst rack seed --- in about 14.5\,ms, and the gated any-shot
ceiling workload --- the same break at the slider-maximum
$v_0 = 12$\,m/s --- in about 16.6\,ms, while whole-table state
sampling for rendering costs about 5\,$\mu$s per call. Per-shot cost is
therefore three to four orders of magnitude below real time for
interactive play, and a full re-simulation is cheap enough to be the
replay and adjudication primitive of
Section~\ref{sec:simulator-determinism}.

The second block is a matched-scenario comparison against the oracle of
Section~\ref{sec:oracle}: the 30-shot ORACLE-X \expid{A3} grid plus the
five committed oracle fixtures, constructed verbatim under the
parity-convention model registry. On these shots the two engines'
event sequences agree 38/38 \emph{after} the declared time-zero
normalization of Section~\ref{sec:oracle} --- on 15 rolling-strike cases
pooltool additionally emits a zero-duration sliding-to-rolling
transition at the strike instant, which that bookkeeping rule drops from
both streams --- so the timed workloads are sequence-identical up to
that documented rule. Each scenario was timed for 120 repeats after
warm-up on both engines --- the kernel's native build through its Python
binding (binding overhead included), pooltool 0.6.0 at the pinned oracle
commit (Python 3.12, numba 0.62) --- and the entire measurement was run
twice, with run-to-run median drift below 3\% on both engines. The kernel
resolves these shots at 0.025--0.118\,ms per shot at the median
(median of medians 0.064\,ms) against pooltool's 0.75--2.97\,ms (median
1.62\,ms): a per-scenario ratio of 22--30$\times$ (median 26$\times$).
pooltool's steady state deliberately excludes just-in-time compilation,
which is a one-time cost of about 13.7\,s in a cold-cache process (0.15\,s
with its default warm on-disk numba cache); the kernel has no
corresponding warm-up.

Three caveats keep this comparison honest. First, it is a comparison of
implementations on one machine, not of formulations: pooltool is a
JIT-compiled Python package whose stated design priorities include speed
\citep{kiefl2024pooltool}, and its absolute per-shot cost of 1--3\,ms is
itself far below real time; the ratio should be read as characterizing
an ahead-of-time compiled kernel against a JIT-compiled interpreted-host
package, on this host only. Second, the compared scenario set is the
oracle registry's two- and three-ball verification grid, not a
break-heavy workload; kernel break costs appear in the first block of
Table~\ref{tab:perf} and no cross-engine break comparison is made,
because the two engines deliberately differ in break-cluster resolution
(Section~\ref{sec:simulator-break}). Third, for the closest prior art on
the multibody side, \citet{gismerosmoreno2022multiple} report --- on
different hardware, as published --- wall-clock costs of
$2.4\times10^{4}$ to $1.0\times10^{5}$\,s for 3--4\,s of simulated
four-ball play under their full MATLAB formulations (440--1{,}730\,s for
their reduced variants); we cite their numbers as reported and draw no
ratio against them, since neither hardware nor workload is matched. The
architectural point stands independently of any specific figure: closed
forms between events make per-shot cost proportional to the event count
rather than to simulated time, which is what confining numerical
integration to the bounded break episode
(Section~\ref{sec:simulator-break}) preserves.
