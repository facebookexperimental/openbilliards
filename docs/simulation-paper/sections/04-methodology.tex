\section{Verification and validation methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

The experiment battery reported in Section~\ref{sec:results} was designed,
registered, and executed under a single doctrine: comparisons are stated in
ASME V\&V~20 vocabulary \citep{asme2009vv20}, every acceptance criterion was
frozen before any sweep ran, and every miss is recorded in a machine-readable
ledger rather than absorbed by parameter adjustment. This section defines the
vocabulary, the four evidence tiers the anchors draw on, the registration
discipline, the waiver-ledger doctrine, and the cross-simulator oracle
protocol.

\subsection{Vocabulary: ASME V\&V 20}

Every validation anchor declares a validation variable, one or more
validation points, and a comparison error $E = S - D$, where $S$ is the
simulation value and $D$ the source datum \citep{asme2009vv20}. V\&V~20
composes the validation uncertainty from numerical, input, and
experimental-data components,
$u_{\mathrm{val}}^2 = u_{\mathrm{num}}^2 + u_{\mathrm{input}}^2 + u_D^2$;
Table~\ref{tab:uval} states, per source family, how each component is
estimated in this battery, and the paragraphs below give the estimation
procedures honestly, including where a component is zero by construction
and where it cannot be estimated at all.

\emph{Numerical uncertainty.} $u_{\mathrm{num}} \approx 0$ at the scale of
every registered band, and we say why rather than assert it: between
events ball motion is closed form and event times are polynomial roots
polished to machine precision, the kernel's solution-verification floors
measure $10^{-16}$--$10^{-13}$ in the native observables of each channel
(Sections~\ref{sec:results-b}, \ref{sec:results-f}), and the canonical
binary is bit-exact across runs and across every platform--runtime
configuration the determinism matrix actually tests --- V8-based
runtimes (Node, Chrome) on macOS arm64 and Linux x86\_64, native builds
on both operating systems, and the Python-SDK legs the battery itself
ran on (Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}) --- so run-to-run and
cross-platform variance contribute exactly zero \emph{on the tested
configurations}. The claim is scoped to those legs: the matrix's
untested cells (non-V8 engines, wasmtime, Windows) are enumerated in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} and are not covered by it; every battery
output was produced and replay-verified on tested legs. The one
time-stepped component, the compliant break resolver, is step-converged
on its battery observables at both energy scales: the E1 kiss-energy
retention curve (spread $\leq 6.3\times10^{-4}$ under step
halving/doubling) and the E3 break-scale squat and effective-mass
observables (spreads $\leq 2.8\times10^{-4}$ on the squat fraction and
$\leq 7.7\times10^{-4}$ on $M_{\mathrm{eff}}/m$ under the same bracket,
with every evidence-band verdict invariant; both post-hoc sweeps
recorded in the artifact bundle; Section~\ref{sec:results-e}). The
$u_{\mathrm{num}} \approx 0$ statement carries one further scoped
exception: the Mathavan-port cushion family integrates its contact in
$\Delta P$ impulse steps ($N = 10^3$, first-order bias control) rather
than in closed form. Its discretization error is measured directly
rather than proxied: halving and doubling the impulse step around the
shipped defaults moves no scored overlay point by more than
0.0022\,m/s on speed observables or $0.044^\circ$ on angle observables
(recorded maxima 0.0021\,m/s and $0.0431^\circ$ on the overlay's 4-dp
values; the quoted bounds sit one rounding quantum above),
with every per-figure within-tolerance count invariant across the three
step arms (post-hoc convergence sweep recorded in the artifact bundle;
the kernel's own anchor suite independently records $\Delta P$
convergence stable to $2\times10^{-6}$ on its scenario family). The
0.005\,m/s and $0.07^\circ$ residuals of the figure-versus-equations
source-discrepancy row are a different quantity --- the protocol
comparison of the kernel
against the source's \emph{printed equations}, whose pin tolerance is
itself 0.005\,m/s --- and are not a discretization measurement.

\emph{Input uncertainty.} The frozen catalog is a declared point choice,
not a measured distribution, so no prior input uncertainty was propagated
at registration. After registration, one-at-a-time brackets --- $e_b \in
[0.92, 0.98]$ around the declared 0.95, and the $\mu(v)$ source-revision
spread the ledger itself records ---
measure the input-uncertainty response of every headline domain boundary
post hoc; the measured responses are woven into Section~\ref{sec:results}
and Table~\ref{tab:domains}, and the analyses are declared as
post-registration characterization recorded in the artifact bundle (a
pinned sensitivity report), never re-tunes. One scoping
statement applies to the whole sensitivity pack: unlike the battery
itself, whose 16{,}933 recorded simulations replay end-to-end through
the shipped wasm binary (Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}), the
sensitivity scripts ran on the native Python surface only, under the
same source-identity argument the pre-replay battery used (the Python
environment's kernel source-identical to the canonical binary); they are
not part of
the wasm replay corpus, and extending the tap/replay harness to the
sensitivity pack is left as an artifact-maintenance item. Relatedly, the
battery runs one simulation per grid cell by design: the kernel is
deterministic and byte-stable across repeated runs
(Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}), so replicate statistics would measure
exactly zero variance; the error bars available to a deterministic
simulator are grid quantization and parameter sensitivity, and those are
what the brackets report.

\emph{Provenance of $e_b$ and its bracket.} The declared ball--ball
restitution $e_b = 0.95$ is a catalog point choice, and we state its
provenance at the precision it actually has: the value sits at the
center of the 0.92--0.98 phenolic ball--ball range printed, without
attribution, in Alciatore's physical-properties compilation
\citep{alciatore_faq} --- a community constant. We could locate no
systematic first-hand measurement behind that range: the book-length
treatment the community often associates with such constants
\citep{marlow1995physics} is out of print and reached us only through
secondary quotations, and the Marlow discussions we could verify (e.g.\
in \citealp{mathavan2010cushion}) concern \emph{cushion} restitution,
not ball--ball impacts. No source states an uncertainty for the range,
and the battery's own reference
forms variously idealize $e = 1$ (TP~A-14) or fix $e = 0.94$ (TP~A.5's
own evaluation). The bracket endpoints $[0.92, 0.98]$ therefore coincide
with the compilation's range but are a \emph{declared
sensitivity range}, not a measured interval. One modeling asymmetry is
deliberate and should be visible: $e_b$ is held speed-constant from the
battery's slowest throw-sweep impacts (0.45\,m/s ball--ball speed,
experiment \expid{A3}) to its fastest break impacts (12.9\,m/s,
experiment \expid{E3}) while the cushion channel carries a velocity
hook $e_c(v)$ --- we know of no published pool measurement that either
supports or contradicts an $e_b(v)$ slope at break speeds. What the
artifact can measure is constant-$e_b$ sensitivity, and we scope it as
exactly that: re-running \expid{E3} with every contact held at each
point of a 62-point grid over $[0.92, 0.98]$ moves the squat fraction
by up to 0.0201 and $M_{\mathrm{eff}}/m$ by up to 0.0551 --- observed
grid maxima, not interval bounds: the response is non-monotone and
event-discrete in $e_b$, with an interior jump discontinuity near
$e_b = 0.958$ at which one clip-target band membership flips
(Section~\ref{sec:results-e}) --- but a speed-dependent $e_b(v)$, which would mix
effective values across the break's contact spectrum, is not
expressible in the kernel and is not bounded by these constant-$e_b$
runs, so the speed-constancy assumption itself remains a declared
limitation (Section~\ref{sec:results-e}; analysis recorded in the
artifact bundle).

\emph{Source-data uncertainty.} Three distinct procedures produced the
source comparisons, and ``digitization'' honestly applies to only one of
them. (1)~The cushion-impact figures of \citet{mathavan2010cushion} were
pixel-digitized (2026-07-02) from a 600-dpi \code{pdftoppm} render of the
publisher PDF by pixel-cluster and per-column color sampling, calibrated
against each figure's own frame and tick marks, with overlapping markers
merged to cluster centroids; the per-series spreads recorded in the
shipped fixture are $u_D = 0.01$--$0.04$\,m/s on speed observables and
$1.0$--$2.0^\circ$ on angle observables (the Fig.~7a camera points carry
$u_x = u_y = 0.04$\,m/s). No repeat-digitization study was performed; the
recorded values are cluster spreads from the single digitization pass.
(2)~The technical-proof comparisons are \emph{transcriptions}, not pixel
digitizations: each source's printed closed forms, fitted constants, and
tabulated values are transcribed and self-checked against the numbers
printed in the source PDF (every battery script embeds its transcription
check), so their $u_D$ is a transcription floor rather than a pixel
spread. What survives for this family is source-revision drift, which is
ledgered as a source row rather than absorbed as uncertainty: where a
source exists in mutually inconsistent revisions, both readings are
transcription-checked against their own equations (the worked example is
the TP~A.17 revision row, where TP~A.17's printed $\mu(v)$ decay
constant of $0.77$ conflicts with the $1.088$ of the current TP~A-14
revision and of the kernel). The five measured TP~B.28 bank tables are
printed numeric tables transcribed exactly; the source states its
measurements were careful but gives no error bars, so no measurement
$u_D$ can be estimated for them. What the shipped fixture records
instead is \emph{equipment} context: the spread between the two
on-system tables is at most 0.25 diamond per point --- carried as a
brand-agnostic equipment window, with the full five-table envelope as
the brand-bias envelope --- which characterizes table-to-table
variability, not measurement uncertainty.
(3)~High-speed-video observables carry the capture chain's stated
precision --- ${\sim}1$--2\,px centroiding ($\approx 0.5$--$0.8$\,mm at
ball scale) at $320\times240$, with speed \emph{ratios} independent of
the frame-rate assumption --- and are consumed as evidence bands, not
point data. For rule-of-thumb lore anchors (the FAQ charts and
practitioner systems: the trisect multiplier, the ten-times-fuller flat
retention, the corner-five corrections, the jump chart), the sources
state no uncertainty and no measurement conditions, so
$u_{\mathrm{val}}$ \emph{cannot be estimated}; those rows are labeled
lore in Table~\ref{tab:domains}, and their comparisons are
characterizations against the rule as printed, not validation with
quantified uncertainty. Finally, one anchor family carries a composite
\emph{acceptance tolerance} that is deliberately not an uncertainty:
experiment \expid{C4} accepts a point when $|E| \le u_y + 0.05\,|D|$,
where $u_y$ is the recorded digitization uncertainty and the
5\%-of-$|D|$ term is an acceptance allowance inherited verbatim from the
kernel's continuous-integration anchor suite; the two terms are reported
separately wherever quoted, and the composite is never called
$u_{\mathrm{val}}$.

Verification is kept strictly separate from validation, and the anchors
themselves are classified so that the word ``validation'' cannot lean on
sources that are not measurements. Every anchor carries one of three
classes, printed per rule in Table~\ref{tab:domains}: a \emph{measured
datum} (an instrumented measurement: the Mathavan Fig.~7a camera points,
the five TP~B.28 bank tables, the high-speed-video observations, the
Platinum robot band); a \emph{derived idealized reference} (a source's own
closed form, construction, or numerical model output: the TP~3.3
frictionless-elastic peak, the gearing and trisect constructions, the
Mathavan Figs.~8--10 numerics); or \emph{rule-of-thumb lore} (practitioner
systems and FAQ charts with no stated conditions). We reserve
\emph{validation} for measured-datum comparisons. Comparisons against
derived references are \emph{model benchmarking} against an idealized
form; and where the kernel implements the reference's own model verbatim,
the comparison is \emph{verification of the port} --- the central Group~A
case, since the kernel's catalog implements TP~A-14's fitted $\mu(v)$
verbatim, making the \expid{A3} master-curve comparison
code-against-its-own-source-model (Section~\ref{sec:results-a}); the same
classification governs \expid{C4}'s headline count
(Section~\ref{sec:results-c}). Verification rows in the classic sense
compare the code against its own closed forms or against the
cross-simulator oracle: the internal worksheet forms embedded in the
outputs of experiments \expid{A1}, \expid{A3}, \expid{A4}, \expid{A6},
\expid{A7}, and \expid{E2} (closed-form re-derivations of the kernel's own
impact laws), the \expid{D1} tangent-frame criterion, the \expid{C3}
mirror-symmetry check, and both ORACLE-X ledger rows. Under the
classification, the battery's validation content proper anchors to the
measured class --- the TP~B.28 envelope (\expid{C1}), the Mathavan Fig.~7a
camera points (\expid{C4}), and the frame-tracked high-speed-video bands
(\expid{E2}, \expid{E3}). The robot band (\expid{B1}/\expid{B2}) is
measured but consumed as \emph{in-sample calibration consistency}, not
independent validation: the catalog's per-shaft end masses were derived
from the same Platinum pivot measurements
(Section~\ref{sec:results-b}). Applying the measured-only rule strictly
also reclassifies two rows earlier drafts filed as measured:
\expid{E1}'s numeric datum is the instructional retention constant 0.10
(the video documents the scenario, not a measured retention), and
\expid{F3} carries no numeric video anchor (its airborne-cut reference
is analytic contact geometry) --- both are benchmarking. They join the
\expid{A2} peak band, the trisect multiplier, and their kin as
benchmarking against derived forms and lore, labeled as such wherever
quoted. Verification of the numerical
core itself (quartic event detection, the terminal-rest round-trip law) is
described with the simulator in Section~\ref{sec:simulator}.

One boundary of the standard deserves an explicit statement. V\&V~20
quantifies validation uncertainty \emph{at} the validation points and
declares extrapolation beyond them out of scope: the standard declines to
certify model behavior where no measurement exists. That excluded
question---over what range of inputs an empirical law keeps holding---is
precisely this paper's thesis. The battery is therefore designed to sweep
across each rule's presumed domain and to report where $E$ grows beyond the
registered bands, turning the standard's scoping boundary into the object of
study.

\subsection{Four-tier anchor discipline}

Each acceptance criterion anchors to one of four evidence tiers:
(i)~published sources---further classified per anchor, under the taxonomy
above, as measured data (the Mathavan Fig.~7a camera points, the five
measured through-diamond bank tables of TP~B.28 \citep{alciatore_tp_b28})
or derived reference forms (the transcribed technical-proof master curves
and constructions \citep{alciatore_tp_a14}, the Mathavan Figs.~8--10
numerics \citep{mathavan2010cushion});
(ii)~high-speed-video catalog observations \citep{alciatore_hsv_catalog};
(iii)~a published specification window---the WPA lag criterion of 4.0--4.5
table lengths, which earlier drafts of this program filed as
``first-party table-session data'' and which we correct here: it is a
specification anchor, not a measurement of ours, and the first-party
table-session captures remain staged under quality assurance
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}) and are consumed by \emph{no} registered
anchor in this battery; and (iv)~a cross-simulator oracle, pooltool
\citep{kiefl2024pooltool} at a pinned commit, described below. Tiers and
classes are declared per anchor, so a reader can weight each verdict by
the strength of its source.

The discipline is deliberately anti-overfit: the default constants the
battery scores were frozen before any battery sweep ran --- unchanged
since their 2026-07-01 introduction, with the last calibration touching
the family nineteen days before registration; the per-literal
chronology, including the younger and not-static equipment-catalog
registry, is stated in Section~\ref{sec:methodology-registration} ---
and one parameter set must satisfy independent datasets simultaneously. ``Out-of-sample'' is scoped precisely in
Section~\ref{sec:methodology-registration}: it means no re-tuning against
any battery observable, not blindness, since most anchors pre-existed the
registration as continuous-integration tests. The worked example is
experiment \expid{F1} (slot F13, Fig.~\ref{fig:F13}): the same cloth
catalog must reproduce both the lag window (a pass) and the bank-table
dataset (a registered miss of the frozen all-six criterion), with
neither dataset re-tuned against the other --- quantified once, in
Section~\ref{sec:results-f}. Where the joint requirement fails for a
fitted preset, the failure is ledgered as a validity domain rather than
repaired by re-tuning. Source-data
uncertainty is recorded per anchor under the per-source procedures and
magnitudes of Table~\ref{tab:uval}: pixel-digitization spreads for the
Mathavan figures, transcription floors for the technical-proof forms and
tables, capture-chain precision for the high-speed-video bands.

\subsection{Frozen registered designs}
\label{sec:methodology-registration}

Every experiment received a registered design---sweep grid, measured output,
and acceptance or reporting criterion---committed on 2026-07-21,
\emph{before} any sweep ran. The registered battery comprises 24 experiments
in six groups (\expid{A1}--\expid{A7}, ball--ball impact and throw;
\expid{B1}--\expid{B4}, stick--ball; \expid{C1}--\expid{C4}, cushion and
diamond systems; \expid{D1}--\expid{D3}, curved paths;
\expid{E1}--\expid{E3}, frozen contacts and the break;
\expid{F1}--\expid{F3}, kinematics closed forms and the jump) plus the
ORACLE-X cross-run, i.e.\ 25 pinned outputs. Roughly 60\% of the registered
rules were already covered by standing continuous-integration anchor tests;
those experiments promote the anchors to figure grade rather than re-derive
them, and anchors whose calibration targets fail are retained as documented
ignored gates---never deleted---with each failure carried as a ledger row
(e.g.\ the steep rolling-bank and Han banking targets of
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

Registration provenance is stated here from the version-control history,
because several of its facts qualify the out-of-sample claim.
(1)~\emph{Defaults with the first anchors; the registry after them;
both long before registration.} The four default constants the battery
scores ($\mu_s = 0.20$, $\mu_r = 0.01$, $e_b = 0.95$, cushion
$e_0 = 0.85$) entered the kernel as literature-sourced defaults at the
kernel scaffold commit (2026-07-01), the same day the
first continuous-integration anchor suite --- including the lag anchor
--- was created; the banking anchor suite followed
two days later (2026-07-03). Checked per literal
against the version history, none of the four default literals has
changed since that introduction; later commits only added preset
entries alongside them (fitted cushion presets, 2026-07-02; cloth
presets, 2026-07-04). The \code{EquipmentCatalog} registry that
packages these constants is younger and was \emph{not} static: it was
introduced on 2026-07-04, three days \emph{after}
the first anchors, and its entries were revised through 2026-07-12
(catalog v8) --- nine days before registration --- so
the freeze claim is scoped to the four battery-scored defaults, not to
the registry as a whole (a post-registration catalog v9 added two
visual-variant ball sets whose physics parameters byte-copy their
existing class). One catalog value is explicitly anchor-informed: the
flat rail-friction intercept $\mu_w = 0.20$ was pinned by a 2026-07-02
calibration whose hard constraint was the kick-anchor windows
--- the same anchor family that experiment \expid{C3}
later promotes to figure grade --- so \expid{C3}'s verdict is
calibration-consistent rather than out-of-sample, and its results
paragraph says so. None of the four defaults (nor $\mu_w$) changed
between that calibration and the 2026-07-21 registration --- nineteen
days --- and none has changed since.
(2)~\emph{Blind versus anchor-informed bands.} The registration index
labels every design's prior coverage, and we restate the split here
rather than leave it at ``roughly 60\%'': six designs promote standing
anchors (\expid{A3}, \expid{B1}, \expid{C1}, \expid{C3}, \expid{C4},
\expid{F1}), eight extend partial coverage (\expid{A2}, \expid{B3},
\expid{C2}, \expid{D1}, \expid{D3}, \expid{E3}, \expid{F2}, \expid{F3})
--- fourteen of 24 --- and ten were registered blind, with no prior
kernel evidence on the observable (\expid{A1}, \expid{A4}--\expid{A7},
\expid{B2}, \expid{B4}, \expid{D2}, \expid{E1}, \expid{E2}). For the
anchored and partial rows the registered bands were set by authors who
already knew how the kernel scored against the standing anchors:
``frozen before any sweep ran'' is exact for the battery's sweeps, but
it is not blindness for those fourteen rows, and both halves of the
flagship one-parameter-set exhibit of Section~\ref{sec:results-f} (lag,
banks) pre-existed as anchors during kernel development. What the freeze
certifies is narrower and checkable: no catalog parameter and no
acceptance band moved after registration, including after misses were
measured. (3)~\emph{The 22-to-24 amendment.} The registration index was
drafted at 22 designs; \expid{A6} and \expid{A7} were added during the
same registration pass, and the index as first committed (2026-07-21)
already carries all 24 --- the ``22-experiment'' count survives in that
commit's message and in an internal planning document, and we disclose
the amendment here. No design, \expid{A6}/\expid{A7} included, was
registered after any sweep of any experiment had run: the first battery
scripts landed 43 minutes after the registration commit, and the
\expid{A6}/\expid{A7} scripts the following day. (4)~\emph{Attestation
boundary.} All of these timestamps are commits in an author-controlled
development repository whose history is not public (the public
repository's history begins at a later snapshot); this is \emph{internal}
registration, not an external timestamping service, and a reader must
weigh it as self-attested. We state the dates (registration,
2026-07-21; battery-script commits, two on 2026-07-21 and three on
2026-07-22), and we scope every out-of-sample claim in this paper to
what this history supports.

Deviations from a registered design are themselves declared and ledgered
rather than silently absorbed. Two worked examples: experiment \expid{A4}
registered an english grid of $-100..100\%$ but executed $0..100\%$ in steps
of 2.5\%---sign-symmetric up to squirt direction, so the negative arm adds
no information on the locus under study---declared in the output's parameter
block; experiment \expid{E3} executed the
elevation subset $\{0, 3\}^\circ$ of its registered $0$--$5^\circ$ range and
realized its registered ``momentum-theft baseline'' as the kernel's own
sequential-impulse legacy arm, the external hack being a documented rejected
alternative.

Every experiment script emits a self-describing output: the kernel version
hash, the full shot parameters, fixture identifiers, and a canonical
event-log hash, following the FAIR and FAIR4RS principles
\citep{wilkinson2016fair,chuehong2022fair4rs}. The complete mapping from
figures and tables to pinned outputs is given in
Supplementary Material Section~S1. When the kernel itself changed
mid-program (the two inherited-defect fixes discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:oracle}), the entire battery was re-baselined under the new
pin with per-row drift characterized on byte-verified endpoints, rather than
selectively re-run (Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}).

\subsection{Waiver-ledger doctrine}

V\&V~20 is explicit that validation is not a pass/fail exercise
\citep{asme2009vv20}: a missed band with a characterized mechanism is a
result, not a failure to be engineered away. The battery operationalizes
this with a machine-readable waiver ledger (38 rows at the time of
writing). Each row
records the anchor identifier, the observed delta against its tolerance, a
physical rationale, a disposition, a tracking reference, and the kernel
identity whose artifacts produced its numbers. Nothing is ever silently
re-tuned: a registered band that is missed stays missed in the record, with
the miss quantified. Table~\ref{tab:waiver-categories} summarizes the nine
row categories.

The ledger's maintenance law keeps it honest over time: row identifiers are
stable and rows are never deleted; a fixed deviation moves to the
resolved category with the resolving reference retained; and when a ledger
\emph{evaluation} is itself found wrong, the row is recategorized in place
with the correction recorded inside it (two such corrections exist in the
ledger, one where the original entry had scored the wrong observable
and one where a one-sided evidence bound had been misread as a two-sided
band).

One decision rule separates repair from disclosure, and it was applied
uniformly. A deviation is \emph{fixed} only when root-cause analysis
identifies a mechanism error --- the implementation demonstrably deviating
from its own intended mathematical law --- and every fix must land behind
a preserved legacy option, carry written predicted shifts registered
before the re-run, and trigger a full battery re-baseline
(Section~\ref{sec:oracle}). A miss with no such diagnosed mechanism ---
the model disagreeing with external data or lore --- is \emph{ledgered}
and never re-tuned. A discrepancy traced to a defensible derivation or
convention choice rather than an error is recorded as a convention row,
not fixed; the permanent oracle parity pin is the standing example. Applied to the
nine resolved rows: two are mechanism fixes under the rule
(the slip-law defect, a mis-scaled Coulomb-cap slip impulse inherited
from the reference lineage; the branch-selection defect, a
numerically discontinuous branch selection at a structural double root);
six are script-bookkeeping re-pins that changed no measured column; and one is a
ledger-evaluation correction in which the original row had scored the
wrong observable. The seven
registered-band misses stayed misses because none has a diagnosed
mechanism error: the banking direction reversals, for instance, are
candidate model-form limits of the affected presets, not identified
implementation defects, and they remain ledgered with exactly that
framing (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

Two rows illustrate how the doctrine reads in practice, each quantified
once in Section~\ref{sec:results}. First, the registered \expid{C1}
criterion requires all six through-diamond bank aims inside the measured
TP~B.28 envelope; the default cushion model is inside on five of six,
reported as a miss under the frozen criterion even though the shortfall
is smaller than the source's own table-to-table equipment spread ---
context that is recorded, not invoked as $u_{\mathrm{val}}$ to excuse
the criterion (Section~\ref{sec:results-c}). Second, the same mechanics turn misses
into findings: the trisect-rule row, a
registered-band miss in ledger terms, is simultaneously the paper's
flagship domains-of-validity result
(Section~\ref{sec:results-f}). This stance follows the
non-pass/fail reading of V\&V~20 \citep{asme2009vv20}, and it is consistent
with the value that artifact-review practice places on documented
non-reproduction (the ACM artifact review and badging framework) and with
CODECHECK's principle of recording, rather than fixing, discrepancies found
during independent execution. The open rows are restated as limitations in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Oracle protocol}

Wherever the two simulators' model registries coincide, pooltool
\citep{kiefl2024pooltool} at a pinned commit serves as a cross-simulator
oracle (experiments \expid{A3}, \expid{A6}, \expid{D1}, and
\expid{F1}/\expid{F2}; the matched registry runs Han-model cushions only).
The comparison is event-by-event: both simulators emit their event streams
for identical shot parameters, and event times, event sequences, and
post-event states are compared point by point. The registered tolerances are
$\leq 1\,\mu$s on event times and $\leq 0.1$\,mm on rest positions where
models coincide. Exceedances are governed by waiver bounds, not discretion:
the oracle suite carries an event-level waiver fixture with per-observable
ceilings, and every tolerance exceedance must name an attributed
oracle-side or convention-side mechanism to be waived. The measured
agreement, and the six waived exceedances with their single attributed
oracle-side mechanism, are reported in
Section~\ref{sec:oracle} (slot F14, Fig.~\ref{fig:F14}).

One convention rule is permanent: oracle parity is pinned to the
\emph{legacy} slip law (the convention pooltool shares by inheritance,
corrected in our kernel by the slip-law fix), keeping the oracle channel a
like-for-like verification instrument across kernel evolution
(rationale and the measured convention
gap in Section~\ref{sec:oracle}).
