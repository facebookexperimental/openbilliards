\section{Cross-simulator agreement}
\label{sec:oracle}

The battery's fourth anchor tier is an independent simulator. We use
pooltool \citep{kiefl2024pooltool} at a pinned release (version 0.6.0,
commit \code{9a8abfe0}) as a cross-simulator oracle wherever the two model
registries coincide, and we state at the outset that every comparison in
this section is \emph{verification} in the ASME V\&V~20 sense
\citep{asme2009vv20}: code against code, under matched models and identical
inputs. The oracle harness (ORACLE-X) re-executes the registered
\expid{A3} and \expid{A6} designs --- 38 matched shots --- through both
engines and compares event times, event sequences, post-impact observables,
and rest states point by point (Fig.~\ref{fig:F14}).

\subsection{Agreement under the parity convention}

ORACLE-X feeds both engines identical initial states and strike parameters
and matches the model registries exactly: the same ball parameters on both
sides, the Han cushion family \citep{han2005dynamics}, the same Alciatore
$\mu(v)$ ball--ball friction form \citep{alciatore_tp_a14}, and matched
stick--ball models. One pin is deliberate and permanent. pooltool's
ball--ball resolver carries the pre-fix slip-impulse convention (the case
study below), so the kernel side of every oracle run explicitly selects
the retained legacy law rather than the corrected default. We call this
the \emph{parity-convention law}: oracle parity is defined against the
legacy slip law permanently, which makes the oracle harness a
fixed-convention verification instrument rather than a moving target.

Under that convention the two engines agree at the numerical floor of the
comparison. Across all 38 points, ball--ball impact times agree to
$7.4\times10^{-14}$\,s at worst; throw angles to
$\max|E| = 1.12\times10^{-4}\,^\circ$; post-impact object-ball spin
$\omega_z$ to $1.9\times10^{-4}$\,rad/s; and post-strike cue-ball states
to $5.2\times10^{-11}$\,m/s. Event sequences match 38/38 after one
declared bookkeeping rule: transitions within $10^{-9}$\,s of the strike
are dropped from both streams, with the drop count recorded per point.
The rule exists because the rolling strike sits exactly on the
$\omega R/v = 1$ classification boundary, where pooltool emits a
zero-length sliding-to-rolling transition at ${\sim}6\times10^{-17}$\,s
that the kernel folds into the strike post-state; this occurs in 15 of
the 38 runs and is bookkeeping, not dynamics.

Six tolerance exceedances remain, and all six carry one attributed
oracle-side mechanism. Four final rest positions exceed the 0.1\,mm gate
(worst case 0.185\,mm) and two rollout durations exceed even the
$2\times10^{-4}$\,s waiver ceiling (worst case $8.9\times10^{-4}$\,s),
exclusively on shots that roll out for up to ${\sim}7.5$\,s. pooltool
re-seats ball positions by ${\sim}1\,\mu$m (its documented
\code{make_kiss} step) before resolving a ball--ball contact; the kernel
resolves at the exact quartic contact point and never re-seats, so the
micrometer-scale perturbation is amplified through the cushion and roll
legs of long rollouts. This is a documented convention difference, not a
physics disagreement, and it is waived with exactly that attribution.

Finally, the harness cross-references its shared points against the
registered battery outputs, which ride the corrected default. That
comparison is scoped as a \emph{measurement} of the slip-law convention
gap,
never as an identity gate: the maximum throw difference on the 30 shared
\expid{A3} points is $0.1301^\circ$, the maximum $\omega_z$ difference on
the 8 shared \expid{A6} points is $0.1783$\,rad/s, and stick-branch
points coincide. These numbers \emph{are} the slip-law shift, by design
(panel~(d) of Fig.~\ref{fig:F14}).

\subsection{Case study: two defects found by the methodology}

A useful test of a verification and validation methodology is whether it
finds defects its authors did not know were there. The battery's first
full run found two.

\paragraph{The slip-law defect (ball--ball slip impulse)}
Experiment \expid{A1}'s slip-law characterization recovered a friction
ratio of 0.97424 against the Coulomb-cap form of the technical-proof
derivations \citep{alciatore_tp_catalog}, within $1.2\times10^{-4}$ of
the value $2e_b/(1+e_b) = 0.97436$ predicted if the friction impulse were
mis-scaled --- slip-regime throw ran approximately 2.6\% low, an offset
subsequently visible across the \expid{A3}, \expid{A4}, \expid{A6},
\expid{D1}, \expid{E2}, and \expid{F3} channels. The root cause: the
kernel scaled the ball--ball slip friction impulse with the post-impact
normal velocity difference, $\mu\,e_b\,v_n$ --- a convention inherited
from the reference-implementation lineage the kernel shares with its
oracle. The Coulomb cap instead requires the normal impulse per unit
mass, $\mu\,(1+e_b)/2\,v_n$, a $+2.6$\% relative slip-impulse difference
at $e_b = 0.95$, worth about $0.12$--$0.13^\circ$ of throw at a slow
half-ball hit. The corrected law is now the kernel default ---
slip-regime throw is exactly $\arctan\mu$, the reference form of
\citet{alciatore_tp_a14} --- with the legacy law retained behind an
explicit option.

\paragraph{The branch-selection defect (settled-rebound branch selection)}
Independently, the \expid{C1} bank grid showed through-diamond arrival
discontinuities of 0.49--0.85 diamonds at mid aims under the
Stronge-compliant cushion preset \citep{stronge2018impact}, with no
intermediate arrivals. The root cause: the model's terminal stick-to-slip
equation was solved by a whole-bracket root finder, and at the structural
double root at stick onset the bracket converged onto whichever solution
family $10^{-16}$-scale floating-point noise favored. Sliding and stun
cushion contacts near that tangency therefore jumped between rebound
families (the tangential ratio stepping from 0.60 to 0.74) as the aim
varied smoothly. The fix selects the first crossing by a deterministic
scan and is continuous by construction; the legacy selection is likewise
retained behind an explicit option.

Neither defect was findable by oracle agreement. The slip-impulse
convention is shared with the oracle by inheritance, so ORACLE-X agrees
at the $10^{-14}$\,s level on both sides of the fix; and the
matched-registry oracle runs exercise only the Han cushion family, never
the Stronge branch. The independent published-curve anchors were
load-bearing --- which is the point of a multi-source battery, and, in
our reading, the strongest single piece of evidence for contribution C2.

all seven confirmed. Table~\ref{tab:predicted-shifts} extracts the two

\paragraph{Legacy preservation}
Both pre-fix laws remain available behind explicit options, with
byte-replay proofs: of the 14 archived event streams re-simulated on the
corrected binary, 9 are byte-identical, and the 5 registered
slip-law-class divergences each reproduce their pre-fix stream
byte-identically under the explicit legacy options
(Table~\ref{tab:determinism}, rows 5 and 10). Model evolution is thereby
held to the same determinism doctrine as the shipped binary itself: a
superseded law is not deleted but pinned, and its historical outputs
remain reconstructible to the byte.

\subsection{Verification/validation boundary in this section}

To restate the boundary: everything in this section is verification ---
code against code, or code against its own closed forms --- in the
vocabulary of ASME V\&V~20 \citep{asme2009vv20}. No validation claim in
this paper is derived from oracle agreement; validation against physical
measurement and published data lives in Section~\ref{sec:results}, and
the parity-convention law above exists precisely to keep the two from
contaminating each other.
