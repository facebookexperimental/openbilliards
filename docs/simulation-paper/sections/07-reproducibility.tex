\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:reproducibility}

This section states the reproduction contract behind every number and
figure in the paper, the evidence that the contract holds, and what of
it the public source release supports. The contract is deliberately
narrow and mechanical: one canonical binary, one manifest of expected
hashes, and hash equality as the verification criterion.

\subsection{Source availability}
\label{sec:availability}

The kernel, its WebAssembly and Python bindings, the TypeScript SDK, and
the application that consumes them are published as source in the
project's public repository under the MIT license; the portions of the
kernel derived from pooltool \citep{kiefl2024pooltool} remain under the
Apache License~2.0, and the repository's notice files identify the
upstream project and list the derived files. The public repository
receives curated snapshots of the project's development repository, and
its history begins at a snapshot later than the kernel revision this
paper reports on. Two limits follow, and we state them plainly. First,
the canonical kernel of record, \code{0.1.0+fb3425ad}, is not part of
the public history: a build from the public tree is a later kernel
revision, the hashes and digests quoted in this paper cannot be
regenerated bit-for-bit from it, and this paper reports no measurement
made on it. Second, the canonical \code{pool_wasm} binary of record
(SHA-256 prefix \code{5e3b6ee4}) with its Python wheel and npm SDK
tarball, the experiment scripts, the oracle harness, the 25 pinned
outputs, the expected-hash manifest, the waiver ledger, and the
validated-scenario video pack described below belong to the artifact
bundle, which is not part of the public release and has not been
published. The registered results quoted in this paper are those of the
\code{0.1.0+fb3425ad} battery, and checks made on later canonical stamps
state their stamp (the lineage notes below; Table~\ref{tab:determinism}).

\subsection{Tier-1 protocol}

The reproduction protocol's first tier is designed as \emph{anyone,
minutes --- execute the canonical artifact}; until the bundle is
published, it can be executed only within the project. Its bundle
contains the pinned canonical \code{pool_wasm} binary, whose
version-hash string is identical to the stamp recorded in replay
files, together with the scripts, fixtures, expected hashes, and a
results map tying every figure and table in this paper to the script
that regenerates its data. The bundle also carries the
validated-scenario video pack: sixteen slow-motion renders with
predicted-overlay annotations, each replaying a solved strike
through the then-current shipped canonical binary with its pinned
record digest reproduced \emph{before} any frame is captured
(per-entry kernel stamps recorded in the pack's gate reports),
accompanied by per-entry machine-readable gate reports and a pack
ledger carrying per-entry SHA-256 hashes of the delivered bytes
together with the render and verification history, including the
cue-model provenance
(presentation methods in Supplementary Material Section~S6); the
videos present verified records, and no claim of this paper rests on
them. Verification \emph{is} hash equality: the
reproducer's success criterion is a byte-for-byte match, not a visual
one. The entire kernel of Fig.~\ref{fig:F1} ships as this single
hash-identified binary.

A second tier --- rebuilding the kernel from source --- exists through
the public repository, with a narrower scope than tier~1. The shipped
WebAssembly binary remains the paper's normative physics definition, a
posture with precedent in deterministic-WebAssembly runtimes that treat
the hash-identified binary as the consensus artifact
\citep{wasm2015nondeterminism}. Native and PyO3 builds are development
conveniences: they are held to the wasm by byte comparison where we run
them (Table~\ref{tab:determinism}), but the \code{.wasm} alone defines
expected outputs. A rebuild from the public source therefore lets a
reader inspect and test the published models, not regenerate this
paper's hashes, which are bound to \code{0.1.0+fb3425ad}
(Section~\ref{sec:availability}). The costs of this posture are discussed
in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

WebAssembly can carry this obligation because its numeric semantics are
IEEE-deterministic up to two specified escape hatches --- NaN payload
bits and relaxed SIMD \citep{wasm2015nondeterminism} --- and the kernel
excludes both by construction: simulation state is NaN-free by an
invariant enforced at the API boundary, and the binary uses no SIMD and
no threading. Every transcendental function is compiled into the binary
rather than imported from the host, so engine-to-engine variance in
host math libraries never enters the physics. Replay is version-hashed
end to end, and model evolution is held to the same doctrine: an
archived corpus of event streams re-simulates byte-identically on the
committed binary, with the five streams that predate the corrected slip
law reproducing their pre-fix bytes only under the explicit legacy
options (Table~\ref{tab:determinism}, row 5; Section~\ref{sec:oracle}).

The bundle is designed to the FAIR principles \citep{wilkinson2016fair}
and their research-software profile \citep{chuehong2022fair4rs}: findable
and accessible once published, interoperable
(self-describing JSON outputs carrying kernel version, shot parameters,
and fixture identifiers), and reusable (per-fixture provenance
blocks). The bundle inventory and the step-by-step five-step
procedure are the mechanics of Supplementary Material Section~S2.

\subsection{Baseline identity and the two-sided proof}

The paper's baseline of record was recorded under the canonical wasm
stamped \code{0.1.0+fb3425ad}; the battery outputs themselves were
produced by a native Python-surface build stamped \code{0.1.0+a321d64f}.
The bridge between the two stamps is a source-identity proof, not an
assumption: the version-control diff between the two pins over every
compiled kernel source is empty (the only intervening changes are
test-only files not compiled into the library), and the rebuilt
environment passes all 13 smoke checks. The bridge is no longer
load-bearing on its own: every kernel simulation the battery executes
has since been replayed end-to-end through the shipped binary itself
with digest equality (matrix row 11 below). Four lineage notes apply: after
the baseline was recorded, the canonical stamp advanced to
\code{0.1.0+c1c3de9a} through a catalog-only change (two visual ball-set
entries, with fixture re-pins proving the event streams byte-identical),
and subsequently to \code{0.1.0+8efd660e} through the post-registration
input-envelope revision (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}), proven
bit-exact for every previously
accepted input at its repin --- two supersessions that leave the physics
definition of record for every simulated battery input unchanged (the
\expid{B3} rejection-probe disclosure is the amendment note of
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}); and thirdly to \code{0.1.0+b4d6e418}
through the pinch-strike revision
--- the first supersession that changes simulated physics, confined to
strikes at cue elevations above $60^\circ$. Its battery effect (the
$65$--$75^\circ$ cells of experiment \expid{D2}) and the materialized
\expid{B3} rejection-probe class are measured and re-baselined in the
study's baseline manifest. Fourthly, the stamp
advanced to \code{0.1.0+e654eb93} through the ball--ball at-contact
window guard --- a detection-correctness fix inside the frozen impact
semantics (an airborne re-collision immediately after a same-instant
compound event went undetected), deliberately shipped without a legacy
option because the pre-fix behavior is a defect, not a model choice. It
is, to date, the only supersession beyond the three above that has
moved any battery output: exactly one trajectory-digest leaf of
experiment \expid{F3}'s verification cell~3, with the cell's verdict
and every measured column unchanged. The drift is carried as a
hash-bound waiver of the continuous-reproduction job and as a numbered
exclusion in the study's ledger, with its census recorded in the
baseline manifest, and is retired at the submission-freeze battery
re-baseline. Later canonical advances are catalog and serialization
repins proven battery-empty, chronicled in the same manifest. In every
case the registered results quoted in this
paper remain those of the \code{0.1.0+fb3425ad} battery.

The baseline manifest is proven two-sided. In one direction, the current
outputs are pinned by their SHA-256 hashes. In the other, the superseded
pre-cascade baseline (build \code{0.1.0+5be7647}) was regenerated from a
clean working tree at its own pin and reproduced all 25 pinned
pre-cascade hashes byte-identically, including the oracle run. Every
drift row in the manifest is therefore a measured old-versus-new
comparison on byte-verified endpoints --- never an inference from hash
inequality.

Raw file hashes are commit-bound by construction: every output embeds the
kernel build stamp, which contains the source-control short hash. The
battery therefore also fingerprints trajectories in a stamp-independent
form: every serialized shot record carries a digest computed with the
version stamp replaced by a fixed token --- a cross-build trajectory
fingerprint that identity checks \emph{compare}, never strip (checker
mechanics in Supplementary Material Section~S2). At the re-record that
established this normalization, a JSON tree comparison excluding digest
fields showed zero differences across all 25 outputs; all 3157 digest
leaves were re-recorded; and the full battery, run twice, produced 25/25
byte-identical outputs across runs.

One measured platform sensitivity is reported deliberately, because it
argues \emph{for} the binary-canonical doctrine rather than against it.
A regression probe that sits intentionally on the structural double
root of the branch-selection defect (Section~\ref{sec:oracle}) fails on
Linux x86\_64 native builds
while passing on macOS arm64 native builds: under the explicit
\emph{legacy} bracket selection, the probe measures a tangent-ratio step
of $2.7\times10^{-5}$ on Linux instead of the ${>}0.1$ jump recorded on
macOS, because code-generation and math-library differences between
native targets flip which solution family the legacy path settles on at
the $10^{-16}$-noise tangency. No shipped-artifact obligation is
breached --- the corrected default is continuous by construction, and the
canonical wasm is one binary that native code-generation variance cannot
reach --- but the probe demonstrates that native rebuilds are not
bit-trustworthy at measure-zero critical points, which is exactly why the
expected outputs are defined by the \code{.wasm}.

The matrix's untested cells are stated as untested, not argued away;
they are enumerated, with the structural argument for why the untested
engine cells are expected to pass, in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}. The
cell that mattered most for reading this paper is now closed by
measurement rather than argument: row 11 replays all 16{,}933 recorded
simulations of the battery end-to-end through the shipped binary with
stamp-normalized trajectory-digest equality (16{,}933/16{,}933), and the
replayed digest set covers all 3{,}157 digest-pinned evidence records
carried in the pinned outputs, so the battery grids no longer rest on
the native surface plus the source-identity bridge. The remaining
reader-facing cell is that no non-author has yet executed the tier-1
protocol. The doctrine is to measure, not argue: each remaining cell is
an open pre-submission item.

\subsection{Continuous reproduction}

In the development repository the battery also runs as a
continuous-integration target; the job and its inputs are not part of
the public release. A scheduled (weekly, plus on-demand)
reproduction job --- deliberately
informational, never a merge gate --- rebuilds the Python surface from
pinned sources with a pinned numerical stack, runs the 13-check smoke
test, executes all 24 experiments plus the oracle harness, and checks
every output for physics identity against the committed normalized
manifest under the normalization above (mask version stamps only;
compare trajectory digests). Where the runner lacks the pinned oracle
environment, the oracle harness records an explicit skip reason in its
output: a recorded skip is data; a silent skip is a failure. The runner
platform is itself part of the baseline identity --- one least-squares
fit in experiment \expid{D1} differs in full precision between BLAS
back ends with unchanged trajectory digests --- so the job runs on the
platform the baseline was recorded on.

Any unwaivered mismatch fails the job. A mismatch can be downgraded only
by a waiver entry carrying an identifier, a physical rationale, and a
tracking reference --- the loader rejects entries missing any of the
three --- and this hash-bound waiver ledger is empty by design at the
baseline of record. The paper-level ledger travels the same way: it
ships verbatim in the artifact bundle, and Section~\ref{sec:limitations}
is organized by its categories, so the paper and the bundle reference
one machine-readable object.

Two items close the loop before submission: the wheel and the battery
are rebuilt at the submission-freeze commit, so that raw hash equality
holds with no normalization at all; and a third party (a CODECHECK-style
attestation) executes tier 1 on all three operating systems and confirms
the hashes. Both are recorded above as open cells of the audit matrix.
