\section{Future work}
\label{sec:future}

\paragraph{Reinforcement learning on a validated substrate}
A validated, bit-exact simulator bounds the trust-the-simulator caveat
that runs through learning-era work consuming billiards physics
\citep{fragkiadaki2016learning,xu2025deepphy,memery2025cuetip}, as it ran
through the planner lineage before it
\citep{smith2007pickpocket,archibald2016distributed}. Our
reinforcement-learning program applies the same registration discipline
as this paper's battery --- frozen kernel pins per generation and
byte-reproducible evaluation --- and its generation-1 milestone is
complete: planner-guided policy ranking against the frozen kernel
promoted an incumbent policy under registered gates. The kernel pin
validated in this paper, \code{0.1.0+fb3425ad}, is adopted at the
generation-2 boundary. Those results belong to a second paper; their
relevance here is that contribution C1's validity domains directly scope
the state regions in which trained play can be physics-trusted.

\paragraph{Footage and high-speed-video expansion}
The validation corpus grows next on its measurement side: additional
first-party table-session captures, currently staged under quality
assurance; wider english and speed coverage on the kick/rebound coupling
anchors; and a capture campaign on a K-66 rail profile that would retire
the non-K-66 caveat of Section~\ref{sec:limitations} attached to the
existing high-speed-video anchors \citep{alciatore_hsv_catalog}. Every
new anchor enters through the same gate as the existing battery: design
registered and frozen first, misses ledgered, nothing re-tuned.

\paragraph{Completing the determinism matrix}
Before camera-ready, the audit matrix of
Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility} should be completed rather than
argued. With the full battery now replayed through the canonical wasm
runtime itself (matrix row 11, method H: 16{,}933/16{,}933
digest-identical), the open cells are the remaining engines and
platforms: trajectory hashes on SpiderMonkey, JavaScriptCore, and
wasmtime, and a Windows leg plus the remaining OS/architecture
combinations --- the replay harness already resolves its module graph
through portable \code{file://} URLs, so the Windows leg needs only the
platform, not new tooling. The same pass rebuilds the wheel at the
submission-freeze commit so that raw hash equality holds with no
normalization.

\paragraph{Companion papers}
Two companion manuscripts are planned. A methods paper develops the
compliant frozen-cluster break resolver as a multiple-impact resolution
technique for frozen-contact topologies, in the lineage of
\citet{gismerosmoreno2022multiple} and \citet{cross2023oblique} and of
the contact-force model it builds on \citep{lankarani1994continuous},
with the frozen-pair throw excess
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}) as its opening problem. A short
single-question paper quantifies the 30$^\circ$/90$^\circ$-rule and
diamond-system validity domains for the physics-teaching audience in
which those rules were first studied experimentally
\citep{bayes1963collision,wallace1988billiard,mathavan2009highspeed}.

\paragraph{Community validation loop}
Finally, the artifact is built to be exercised by others: event-level
reproductions of the technical-proof curves form the basis of a planned
outreach and figure-permission workflow with their author
\citep{alciatore_tp_catalog}; a third-party, CODECHECK-style tier-1
attestation on all three operating systems is scheduled before
submission; and cross-simulator work with pooltool
\citep{kiefl2024pooltool} continues in the cooperative framing of
Section~\ref{sec:oracle}, where shared scrutiny has already repaid both
codebases.
