01 — Your first shot

Break, watch,
repeat.

  1. Aim. Left-drag anywhere on the table and the cue swings to point where you drag; a solid white line previews the cue ball's path to first contact. Presses within 2.5 ball radii of the cue ball are ignored, so grabbing too close never knocks your aim off web/app/aim.js.
  2. Set power. Roll the wheel while aiming, or press ↑/↓. Power runs 0.5–12 m/s web/app/aim.js — the cap sits at measured professional break speed (~27 mph).
  3. Refine. Hold Shift and drag for fine aim at 0.1× the pointer's movement; arrow keys nudge ±0.05° (Shift 0.5°).
  4. Shoot. The state banner flips to PLAYBACK — the kernel has already solved the whole shot. When the balls settle, the turn passes: player 1 and player 2 alternate at one machine web/app/flow.js.
  5. Read the HUD. "P1 on the 1" names whose turn it is and which ball is legal — always the lowest number on the table.

03 — Spin & the assist lines

Spin is a point
on the ball.

The spin pad in the lower-left panel is the cue ball's face. Drag the dot: a is side — positive means english to your right — and b is height — above center is follow, below is draw web/app/hud.js. Offsets are measured in ball radii, and the validator enforces a² + b² ≤ 0.25: the tip can never pass the dashed ring at half the radius — the miscue limit. Illegal offsets are simply not settable; there is no in-game miscue event.

The arc slider raises the cue butt from 0° to 80° web/app/aimwidgets.js. Above 45° a MASSÉ hint flashes: expect a curving cue-ball path. The preview runs the same kernel as the shot, so the curve you see is the curve you get.

Three lines to read

  • Solid white — the cue ball's approach, up to first contact.
  • Amber — the object ball's direction after contact, a stub about half a diamond long.
  • Dashed green — the cue ball's path after contact: draw, follow, or the tangent line.

Solid means before contact; dashed means after. That distinction is the whole visual language web/app/preview.js.

Assist levels

  • Off — no lines at all.
  • Standard (path + ghost ball) — the default: approach line, ghost ball at contact, object-ball stub.
  • Practice (all ball paths) — Standard plus the dimmed, fully predicted path of every ball on the table.

The dashed green line has its own switch — "Cue path after contact", on by default web/app/settings.js. All of it re-simulates the real physics kernel on every aim change, in about 0.2 ms per shot web/app/preview.js — the lines are exact physics, not a geometric estimate.

04 — Choosing a game

Five you can play,
each one cited.

The Game picker sits at the top of the Settings drawer with five playable entries: 9-Ball (the default), 8-Ball, Snooker, Three-Cushion Carom and One Pocket. Your choice persists, and switching asks before it acts — "Switching games re-racks the table. Continue?" — because a new game means a new rack (and, for snooker and carom, a different table) web/app/settings.js.

Under the picker, "Game catalog" unfolds the browse view: five disciplines, every one carrying a provenance line that names its governing body and the rulebook edition it is transcribed from — an entry without a real citation is rejected outright sdk/ts/src/games/catalog.ts.

05 — 9-ball rules as implemented

Lowest first.
Nine wins.

A shot is legal when the cue ball's first contact is the lowest-numbered ball on the table and, afterwards, at least one ball is pocketed or reaches a cushion sdk/ts/src/rules/nineball.ts. Anything else is a foul — and fouls can stack:

  • Cue ball hit nothing — no contact at all.
  • Wrong ball first — first contact was not the lowest ball.
  • No rail after contact — nothing pocketed and no ball reached a cushion.
  • Scratch — the cue ball was pocketed or driven off the table.

Any foul hands your opponent ball in hand, anywhere on the table — there is no kitchen restriction. The cue ball reappears on the head spot; drag it wherever you like (placement is checked for overlaps), then shoot web/app/flow.js.

Pocket the 9 on a legal shot and you win — combinations count. If the 9 drops on a foul, or leaves the table, it respots on the foot spot, falling back along the long string; if that line is fully blocked it stays down in v1. A legal shot that pockets nothing passes the turn. A win shows its banner, then auto re-racks with a fresh seed after 2.6 s web/app/flow.js.

Not implemented in v1: the push-out after the break, the WPA dry-break four-balls-to-rail rule, and the three-consecutive-fouls loss. The break is judged like any other shot, and object balls that leave the table stay off — only the 9 respots.

06 — 8-ball rules as implemented

Call everything.
The 8 decides.

8-ball is transcribed from the WPA Rules of Play (eff. 2025-09-15) Sec. 4, and every verdict cites its rule number. Where the rulebook leaves "obvious" shots to a referee, the strictest reading applies instead: every shot is called explicitly, nothing is inferred sdk/ts/src/rules/eightball.ts. Fifteen balls rack in a triangle — the 8 at the center, one solid and one stripe on the rear corners (WPA 4.2).

Open table & groups

The break is the one uncalled shot, and it leaves the table open: the HUD chip reads OPEN TABLE, and first contact may be any ball except the 8 — unless one group is already completely pocketed (WPA 4.4). Groups are decided by the first legally pocketed called ball — pocket a called solid and you are solids (1–7), your opponent stripes (9–15). From then on the chips carry each player's group, flipping to "on the 8" once a group is cleared.

Call your shot

Every shot after the break carries an explicit ball + pocket call (WPA 4.6 via the strictest 1.7 reading). The HUD prompts "Call required: tap a pocket": tap one of the six pocket markers and the indicator confirms it as "Ball N → pocket"; until then, Shoot refuses with "call a ball and pocket first (or declare a safety)" web/app/aim.js. The Safety button next to the spin pad is that alternative (WPA 1.7): declare it and the turn passes after your shot — a ball pocketed on a safety stays down. Make your called ball in its called pocket and you keep the table; anything else passes the turn, though uncalled extras stay pocketed.

Fouls, ball in hand, decisions

The foul list matches 9-ball — scratch, no contact, wrong ball first (here: not your group), no rail after contact — plus a ball driven off the table. A standard foul hands the opponent ball in hand anywhere (WPA 4.9); a break foul restricts placement to behind the head string (WPA 4.3(g),(h)). The foul banner names the rule each time — "FOUL — scratch (WPA 3.1 via 4.9)" web/app/flow.js. There is no three-foul loss in 8-ball (WPA 3.13 excludes itself), and only the 8 is ever respotted (WPA 4.7).

Some breaks end in a rulebook decision instead of a verdict: a prompt names who chooses, shooting stays locked until they do, and the choice is recorded into the replay:

  • Dry break (WPA 4.3(d)) — fewer than four balls reached a rail and nothing dropped: the opponent accepts the table, re-racks and breaks, or re-racks with the breaker re-breaking.
  • 8 on a legal break (WPA 4.3(e)) — the breaker spots the 8 and plays on, or re-breaks.
  • 8 on a fouled break (WPA 4.3(f)) — the opponent spots the 8 with ball in hand behind the head string, or re-breaks.
  • Break foul (WPA 4.3(g),(h)) — the opponent accepts the table or takes ball in hand behind the head string.
  • Stalemate (WPA 4.11) — both players confirm: re-rack, the original breaker breaks.

Winning — and the four ways to lose

Clear your group, call a pocket for the 8, and make it exactly there: that wins the rack (WPA Sec. 4; 4.6). The rack is lost on the spot for pocketing the 8 with a foul on the same shot (4.8(a)), pocketing it before your group is cleared (4.8(b)), sinking it in an uncalled pocket — a safety-potted 8 counts (4.8(c)) — or driving it off the table (4.8(d)) sdk/ts/src/rules/eightball.ts.

07 — Snooker rules as implemented

A red, a colour.
The break builds.

Snooker is transcribed from the WPBSA Official Rules (revised September 2024), and every verdict cites its section. Twenty-two balls take the 12-ft table: fifteen reds packed behind the pink, the six colours on their spots, the cue ball anywhere in the D sdk/ts/src/rules/snooker.ts.

Ball on: red, then a nominated colour

While reds remain, a red is always the ball on (1 point). Pot one and a colour becomes on — tap the colour you nominate (the aim UI highlights the selectable colours and confirms your choice; Shoot refuses until you nominate) web/app/aim.js. A potted colour scores its value — yellow 2, green 3, brown 4, blue 5, pink 6, black 7 — and respots while reds remain; then it is red again, alternating until the reds are gone. The HUD carries both scores, the running break, and a "ball on" chip that always names what is legal web/app/hud.js.

Respots & the final colours

A colour returns to its own spot; if that is occupied it takes the highest-value vacant spot, and failing all six, the nearest point on the centre line toward the top cushion (WPBSA Sec3 R7). The respot queue shows in the HUD and the placement animates. Once the last red's colour is potted, the final colours begin: yellow through black in ascending order, and nothing respots (Sec3 R3(h)(ii)). The frame ends when black falls with the scores unequal; a tie re-spots the black — a toss decides who plays first, and the next score or foul ends it (Sec3 R4) sdk/ts/src/rules/snooker.ts.

Fouls: value of the ball, minimum four

A foul awards the opponent the value of the ball on or the highest-value ball involved, floored at 4 and capped at 7 (Sec3 R11): touch the blue while on a red and it is 5 away — the banner names the rule each time. Fouls cover wrong first contact, no contact, scratch, a ball forced off the table, a missing nomination, and jumping over an intervening ball. After any foul the incoming player may also ask you to play again (Sec3 R13) — a prompt records the choice into the replay. A scratch gives cue ball in hand within the D (Sec3 R8(a)): the placement clamp holds your drag on or inside the D web/app/game.js.

Referee-judgment clauses are deliberately out of scope, documented with their citations rather than invented: foul-and-a-miss (Sec3 R14), the free ball (Sec2 R13), touching ball (Sec3 R9), push stroke (Sec3 R18) sdk/ts/src/rules/snooker.ts.

08 — Three-cushion carom as implemented

Three cushions,
then the second ball.

Three balls, no pockets: the UMB World Rules (1 January 1989) on the 2.84 m heated-slate table. Under the default two-ball setting each player keeps their own cue ball all match — the starter the plain white, the other seat the yellow (Art 22.1/23.2, world-championship practice; striking your opponent's ball is the cited wrong-ball foul, Art 41.11, and the classic shared-white variant stays one click away in Settings). A point requires your cue ball to contact both object balls, touching at least three cushions before it first touches the second (Art 83) — the same cushion twice counts twice, and the order is read straight off the kernel's event stream sdk/ts/src/rules/carom3c.ts. Score and you shoot again; a cannon reached short of three cushions is announced with the rulebook's words — "no cushion" / "one cushion" / "two cushions" (UMB Art 83), a foul that passes the turn — while a clean miss stays the legal miss (Art 24.5). The HUD counts cushions live during each shot, next to points, innings, and the race target web/app/hud.js.

The break, respots, the race

The opening prompt asks which start spot you break from — left or right of the bottom spot (Art 23.2) — and the starting cannon must contact the red first (Art 23.3); anything else is the cited break foul. A ball forced off the table respots on its designated spot — red to top, the incoming player's cue ball to bottom, the other to centre, with the swap rule when a spot is masked — and only the balls concerned move (Art 27.3(b), 28.1(b)). First to the race target (15 by default, configurable in Settings) wins — or, under the equal-innings preset (Art 29.3/29.4), the trailing seat gets one leveling inning from the respotted start position, and a leveled score ends the match in a draw; the result screen shows both players' points and innings (Art 29.1/29.2) web/app/flow.js.

On the pocketless table the game draws no pocket markers and the aim line never snaps to a pocket. Physical-world fouls (touched balls, push-through, feet off the floor; Art 41.4–41.7, 41.9–41.10) stay documented out of scope with their citations, while playing straight into a cushion your cue ball is frozen to is judged (Art 27.2 via 41.8), the wrong-ball foul rides the two-ball setting (Art 41.11), and the equal-innings format is a preset in Settings (Art 29.3/29.4) sdk/ts/src/rules/carom3c.ts.

09 — One-pocket as implemented

One pocket each.
Eight balls win.

One-pocket is transcribed from the WPA Rules of Play (eff. 2025-09-15) Sec. 12: fifteen balls, but each player scores only in their own foot-corner pocket. Before the break, a prompt assigns the pockets — the default follows 12.3, the pocket opposite the side you break from — and badges on the HUD mark whose pocket is whose. Any ball into your pocket counts; first to eight wins sdk/ts/src/rules/onepocket.ts.

Every pocket means something

Balls into your opponent's pocket score for the opponent — even when you foul on the same stroke (12.5). Balls into the four neutral pockets, or driven off the table, come back: they queue as "to spot" chips and return to the foot spot when your turn ends (12.6, spotted immediately if the table is otherwise cleared). The per-pocket tallies live on the HUD next to the owed-ball chips web/app/hud.js.

Fouls, owed balls, three strikes

After most fouls the cue ball stays where it lies (12.8) — only a scratch or cue ball off the table gives the opponent ball in hand behind the head string (3.1). A foul also costs one ball from your score, spotted back to the table; foul at zero and you owe one (12.4) — the HUD shows the debt, and your next scored ball repays it immediately. Three consecutive fouls lose the rack outright (12.9 via 3.13); the counter resets on any legal shot. A stalemate re-racks with both tallies and owed balls carried over, and a lag decision picks who breaks (12.10) sdk/ts/src/rules/onepocket.ts.

What the badges mean

Every catalog entry wears one of three badges, telling you how its physics parameters are grounded:

  • calibrated  — parameters fitted to measured data; drawn as the accent chip in-game.
  • literature  — values taken from published papers and manufacturer specs.
  • estimated  — a documented best estimate, pending measurement.

New in catalog v3: the table axis now carries measured pocket geometry — the Pool tournament 9-ft entry plays its audited 4.5 in corner mouths and 141°/102° facings, measurably tighter than the generic WPA table. the table-dimension audit (internal)

Two quiet behaviors worth knowing: choosing a table silently engages that brand's recommended cushion model, and the equipment pickers lock during a shared replay — you watch on the sender's spec.

11 — Replays & sharing

The link carries
shots, not frames.

"Share replay" packs the entire game into the URL after #r= — the rack seed, every shot's five numbers (v0, φ, θ, a, b) verbatim, each ball-in-hand placement, the full five-axis equipment spec, and the kernel version — compressed, encoded, and copied to your clipboard sdk/ts/src/share.ts.

No frames travel. The viewer's browser re-simulates every shot on the same canonical wasm kernel, and the resulting records byte-match the sender's — that is the determinism guarantee web/app/share.js. If the kernel version differs, a warning offers "View anyway" or "Dismiss".

The transport bar shows shot i/N with previous, next, play/pause and a 0.25× slow-motion toggle; shots play with 700 ms of quiet between them web/app/share.js. While a replay runs, shot input, cue placement and equipment changes are all locked. The link also remembers which game it belongs to — an 8-ball share replays with its groups, calls and break decisions intact, while links from before the game axis (v1–v3) still decode and replay as 9-ball, never prompting sdk/ts/src/share.ts.

  • Continue from here — adopt the final position for live play. The continuation is itself immediately shareable, and you keep playing on the sender's equipment until you reload.
  • Exit replay — restore your own equipment and re-rack.

12 — Video export tiers

Five tiers,
one shot.

"Export video…" re-renders your last shot — it asks you to play one first if there is none. Realtime records live as the replay plays and works in any browser; every other tier is a deterministic offline re-render that needs WebCodecs, encoded in-page as mp4 (H.264/AAC) with a webm (VP9) fallback web/app/export.js.

Bitrates run 8 / 12 / 24 / 40 Mbps at 720 / 1080 / 1440 / 2160p web/app/export.js. Speed offers 1×, 0.5× and 0.25× on offline tiers; sound is a checkbox. Clips cap at 60 s (plus a 0.5 s tail) at 60 fps. The dialog probes ten frames when it opens to refine its ETA and file-size estimates, and resolutions above 1080p require the dedicated UHD encoder probe to pass. A running export stops only through its Cancel button — Escape and backdrop clicks are deliberately ignored. Files download as openbilliards-<tier>-<timestamp>.mp4 or .webm.

13 — Recording & slow-mo

Press record,
keep everything.

"Record" captures the live canvas at 60 fps plus the master audio mix into a webm — VP9 with a VP8 fallback, at 8 Mbps web/app/record.js. It is state-agnostic: aiming, menus, shots — whatever is on screen. A pulsing red dot marks a live recording, and Stop downloads openbilliards-<timestamp>.webm. Chunks are written every second, so even an abrupt tab death keeps the footage. On browsers without support, the button simply never appears.

Slow motion lives in three places: the HUD "slow-mo" checkbox plays every shot back at 0.25× web/app/game.js, the shared-replay bar carries the same 0.25× toggle (it is the same switch), and the export dialog renders 0.5× and 0.25× offline.

14 — Language settings

Seven languages,
switched live.

The game ships in English, 中文, español, 日本語, Deutsch, français and 한국어. It resolves your language in order: the explicit choice in Settings (persisted), then your browser language's best match — zh-TW lands on 中文, es-MX on español — then English web/app/i18n.js. Switching applies live, without a reload.

Every locale carries exactly the same keys — the e2e suite enforces it, and anything missing falls back to English. The language picker's labels stay in their own languages by design. This guide works the same way: the pill row at the top switches between its seven translations, and your choice persists.

15 — FAQ

Surprises,
explained.

I switched tables and the cushion model changed by itself.
Intentional: each table engages its brand's recommended cushion model, without a separate prompt. You can still pick another model under Advanced afterwards.
The wheel won't change my power.
The wheel sets power only while aiming. Outside an aiming state it zooms — and Shift + wheel always zooms.
The help line says Shift-drag orbits, but it fine-aims instead.
While aiming, Shift + left-drag claims the drag for 0.1× fine aim. It orbits only when you are not in an aiming state.
I continued from a shared replay — whose table am I on?
The sender's. "Continue from here" keeps their full equipment spec applied until you reload; "Exit replay" restores yours and re-racks.
I won, and the table instantly re-racked. Can I replay that rack?
The auto re-rack after a win (2.6 s) advances the rack seed, so the next rack is new. The winning rack lives on in Replay and in any link you shared.
The spin dot stops at the dashed ring.
That ring is the miscue limit: tip offsets beyond half the ball radius (a² + b² > 0.25) fail validation and cannot be set. There is no miscue event — the limit is the rule.
Does Replay lose my ball-in-hand placement?
No. Replaying while you hold ball in hand preserves your lined-up cue placement after the replay finishes.
Why does power stop at 12 m/s?
It is a deliberate real-world ceiling, matched to reliably measured professional break speeds (~27 mph) web/app/aim.js. The kernel itself hard-caps validation at 15 m/s.
"Standard" export says film grade, but my clip has no motion blur.
Correct — Standard renders a single subframe. Motion blur and depth of field start at the High tier web/app/export.js.
Where are the push-out and the three-foul rule?
Not in v1. The break is judged like any other shot, dry breaks are not penalized, and object balls that leave the table stay off — only the 9 respots sdk/ts/src/rules/nineball.ts.

Lowest ball first.
Always.
