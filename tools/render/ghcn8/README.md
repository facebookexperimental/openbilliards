## FIX21: a preview restore remains an obligation

ORDER-FIX21 v1.4 replaces the earlier F2'' ruling. R-a restores every key
that a committed successor does not own. R-b records ownership at the write:
an axis change owns that axis and its explicitly written cushion pairing;
a whole-tuple write owns all staged equipment axes. A game write owns the
tuple only when it replaces it; same-game and transient writes own none.
Refused/cancelled requests and a preview's own re-stage acquire no ownership.
R-c restores scalars immediately, including both fixture fields, then removes
their shield bases. R-d defers equipment restoration rather than dropping it.

Deferral is tuple-wide while any eligible equipment/game caller or archive
hold remains. New requests retire older ineligible leases; unresolved retired
transport cannot retain a cancelled preview. Callers settle after their own
guards, rerack and auto-close, including false/superseded branches. There is
no table-state settlement listener. A normal non-persisting setter restores
the remaining keys; only its commit consumes them. A refused restore retains
its debt for a later caller tail or Revert, without a self-retry loop.

Playback takes a JSON-string settings hold at suspend(true), including fits
routes with no swap, and releases it at every suspend(false). Resume also
unwinds on a throw. Null/non-matching releases do nothing. The hold affects
the restoring preview write, not explicit takeovers. Stale settings without
the feature-detected seam retain the previous behavior. active() exposes
reverting keys and the hold; Keep/Stage refuse that phase by name. The Atelier
hides Keep and uses seven-locale copy. OWNED closes also send a paint-only
